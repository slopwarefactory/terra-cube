## Not found under CC0

- Fox bark or yip: nothing CC0 on OpenGameArt or Commons (Commons has only spoken words).
- A true wolf howl: `wolf_1` is a growl and `wolf_2` a short voiced creature howl, not a recording of a wolf.
- Deer: `deer_1` is an elk bugle, the nearest public domain call.
- Blade hitting flesh: `hit_1` is a punch and `hit_2` a knife slice.
- Lake water lapping: `amb_water` is a generic water loop.
- A dedicated monster death sound: `monster_die_*` are a roar and a short monster voice.
- Wind in trees specifically: `amb_wind` is plain mild wind.

Added 2026-11-11, for the air of a place: beds of several recordings each (`amb_wind_1` to `_6`, `amb_water_1` to `_4`, faded one into another at random) and the small sounds heard one at a time over them (a set to each kind of songbird, `far_*` heard from far off, `owl`, `frog_3` on, `insect`, `rustle`). The looped birdsong (`amb_birds`) and the two chirps (`bird_1`, `bird_2`) they replace are gone. All are from the sound libraries of Yellowstone and Rocky Mountain National Parks (US National Park Service; each library's page says its recordings are in the public domain, and asks that the National Park Service be credited where appropriate) and from Wikimedia Commons (only files its licence data gives as public domain or CC0), fetched with a plain client; OpenGameArt was not used, since it refuses automated fetching. The originals are not kept here; `tools/audio/cut.sh DIR` makes every file below again from them (the "Original file" column is the name it expects in DIR), and each row says which stretch of the recording it is. Calls were found by `tools/audio/cut.py` by how far they stand over the quiet of the recording, not by ear: a cut may hold a second bird, or half a phrase. Nobody has listened to any of them.
