# Engine design

Decided 2026-11-09, the first night. Each choice gives its reason so it can be argued with. Evidence: [research/2026-11-09-engine-prior-art.md](research/2026-11-09-engine-prior-art.md).

## The choice: Bevy for the frame, our own crates for the world

**Bevy 0.20, pinned, for windowing, rendering, assets, glTF, skinned animation, input, UI and its ECS. Our own code for everything that is the game: voxels, meshing, simulation, creatures' behaviour.**

Why not our own engine on wgpu:

- We are one person and an agent, and not game developers. A renderer, an asset system, skinned animation with blending and a glTF loader are months of work that Bevy has, and none of it is what makes this game different.
- Every maintained Rust voxel library found targets Bevy. The Rust voxel games that wrote their own renderer (Veloren, Tiny Glade) had rendering specialists or years of volunteers.
- The thing we had to prove first, generated models that animate, needed exactly the parts Bevy ships. It took one evening, not a quarter.

What Bevy costs, and how we contain it:

- **API churn**: a release every three to five months, each breaking. We pin the version, upgrade deliberately at most once a release, and agents read the local examples instead of trusting memory ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), Rules).
- **Lock-in**: kept shallow. Voxel data and meshing (`cube_voxel`) use no Bevy types, and the simulation will not either. If Bevy's renderer ever becomes the limit for terrain, we replace how chunks are drawn (a custom material or render pass) and keep the rest. Tiny Glade did exactly that: Bevy's ECS and app, its own renderer.
- **Custom shaders** break on upgrades (0.20 moved WGSL to WESL). So shaders are for environment effects only, written late, kept few.

## Crates

| Crate | Depends on | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `cube_voxel` | glam | Blocks, chunks (32³), the world map, meshing to plain vertex arrays |
| `cube_sim` | cube_voxel, serde | The simulation at a fixed 60 ticks a second, runnable and testable with no window: making the world from a seed (`terrain`), measuring it (`survey`), how a body moves through voxels (`body`), the creatures and one tick of all of them (`life`), what each decides (`mind`), seeded randomness (`rng`). To come: block changes, needs, saving |
| `cube_game` | bevy, cube_sim, cube_voxel | Drawing and input: it draws what the simulation holds and feeds the player's keys in |
| `world` | cube_sim, image | `cargo world`: the world and its creatures judged with no window, in numbers and one map |
| `forge` | glam, serde, image | The creature pipeline; never linked into the game |

The rule this protects: **the simulation never needs a window.** A big sandbox with heavy simulation will be server-authoritative sooner or later, and a simulation that runs headless is also one an agent can test in milliseconds.

## Voxels

- **A cell is 0.25 m**, a chunk 32³ cells (8 m): a quarter of Minecraft's 1 m, so a hillside reads as a slope and a tree's crown as round, and a 1.75 m player is seven cells tall. Chosen 2026-11-10 from a side-by-side picture against 0.5 m, before block editing exists, when it was cheapest to change. It costs 64 cells a cubic metre against Minecraft's one: the island is 1,500 chunks and 2.1 million faces, about 900 MB at its peak while starting. So storage and meshing are what to watch (palette-compress chunks, mesh greedily), and digging and building will want to work in pieces bigger than a cell. Everything that means a distance (terrain shapes, tree sizes, how high a body steps) is written in metres and turned to cells with `CELL`, so it can change again.
- **Any cell can break into 5 cm bits** (`SPLIT` 5, so 125 to a cell), and only broken cells cost more. Tried 2026-11-10 to answer whether a fine grid could be the rule without the whole world being stored that fine: it can. A cell is whole until something smaller than a cell happens to it (a hole dug in it, something built on it), then it holds its own 125 bits; left all one thing again, it mends. Bodies collide with bits, rays stop at them, and a hole is a rounded bowl, not a missing cube. So 5 cm is the smallest thing in the world and the unit things are placed on, while hills are stored a hundred and twenty-five times cheaper. Measured (`cargo world dig`): a hole 0.3 to 1.6 m across breaks about 35 cells and costs 5 kB; 120 of them add 7% to the faces drawn; a changed chunk is drawn again in under 2 ms; creatures live among them without ending up in the ground. The cost that grows is faces: a broken cell's flat side is 25 little squares where a whole cell's is one, so 5,000 holes on this island triple what is drawn. Merging flat faces (greedy meshing) is the answer and is not built.
- **Meshing today**: one quad per visible face, ambient occlusion baked into vertex colour, slight per-cell tint. No textures. About the simplest thing that does not look like one flat tile.
- **The Eco look is shaped blocks, not smooth isosurfaces**: blocks keep the grid (so building and simulation stay simple) but draw as mesh pieces chosen by their neighbours (bevelled edges, slopes, rounded corners). That is the main lever for "much better than Minecraft" and the next terrain job. Marching cubes or surface nets would look smoother but lose the block grid that building and simulation want.
- **Deep water is harder to see through**: a water face's alpha grows with the water under it, so a ford shows its bed and the sea does not. The sea beyond the island is four plain sheets out past the fog.
- **Not yet**: greedy meshing (measured elsewhere at 50 to 350 µs a chunk for plain meshing, so not urgent), saving. (Meshing off the frame's thread, levels of detail and cells kept only near the player are done: [A big world](#a-big-world).)

### How things move and meet (`cube_sim::matter`, 2026-11-13)

The developer: "our physics engine is key... how weight works like with the tree falling, how everything collides with each other, what you can and cannot just 'walk over', speed of things. should make sure we've ... [made] it well designed and that ill continue to work as we add more and more things to the world." Until then each rule had been written where it was first needed. They are one design, and its numbers are in one module (`matter`, whose head says the whole of it):

- **A great tree is worked like any other, and costs a frame less** (2026-11-13; trees of 23 to 45 m, 50,000 to 86,000 cells, at 10,60 and -32,-40). Felled, the leaf of its crown came loose from it: leaf bears nothing, so each mass of it with a few twigs in it was a body nothing held, fell away from the tree that was still drawn whole and lay as cells nobody could see, and a script's blows waited ten seconds for the last of it. A mass of a felled tree's leaf that its trunk is not in is the tree's and stays (by where its trunk lies, `Thing::lain_trunk`: by how much wood was in it, tried first, a thin tree had no more than a great one's twigs and would not have fallen). And a tree that lies with its boughs on and loses the ground under it comes down again as the tree: its cells came down without it, and it was left drawn in the air. Three costs were found by timing (`LOOSE_TRACE=1` now says of a slow step, `loosen` and `take` where the time went): looking for what is loose began from every cell of the land in the tree's box, 460,000 to 620,000 of them (over a wide place the land is no longer begun from, `WIDE`, and the box is read chunk by chunk); and every map keyed by a cell used the standard hasher (`loose::Cells` is the world's own quick map, `cube_voxel::ByPlace`). The worst frame working such a tree went from 160 ms to 60, and the step it lands in from 97 ms to 34. And a great piece (over 20,000 cells, `SPREAD`) is put into the world a few rows of cells a step when it lands, not all at once (`Loose::put_down`): it stays among the pieces, unmoving, until the last of it is in, so whoever waits for nothing to be falling waits for that too, and its cells cannot be seen coming since it is drawn as the tree. A tree that lies as itself is one body and nothing of it is looked at for having come loose when it lands. A blow among the boughs of a tree that lies unlimbed is at that tree (it was taken for one at timber, which bucked the trunk with its crown on), and limbing takes the tree's bits out of cells in bits too (a hundred such cells at a time were left in the air and fell unseen). `make trees` holds all of this at four places. Left: the blows that fell, limb and buck such a tree are 20 to 30 ms each, once.
- **A script's tool strikes the ground a stride on** (2026-11-14). A script has no eye: its spade or pick struck where the middle of the picture fell, which is within reach only on a hillside it happens to face, so the land's own stone had never been dug in a trial. Where that is out of reach its blow is at the ground a stride before it. `make stone` digs bare rock with the pick so: a bite takes the stone and leaves a hunk, which is carried.
- **A pick breaks a lump of rubble; an axe does not** (2026-11-14). Breaking a lump that lies at rest as itself into hunks was written among what an axe does to timber, so a pick did nothing to one and an axe would have. No trial had struck a lump in the real game: a script's `pick` aimed only at boulders. It aims at the nearest lump when no unbroken boulder is near, and `make shatter` breaks a dropped stone's lumps into hunks (fourteen of a stone a metre and a half across, which is its stone) and carries one.
- **An edit that did nothing is not kept, and a rock is its stones together** (2026-11-13). The world is its seed and its journal, and `Loose::cut` and `chop` wrote every swing into it: sixty blows of a pick at a stone already broken were sixty edits. One that took nothing, made nothing its cells and loosed nothing is now taken out again. And a small rock is often two or three stones lying in each other: breaking one took the stone of the others, which then took their own blows and broke into nothing; they are broken with it, and a rock too small or too sunk for a hunk's place to be free within it sets its hunk down where it lay. `make stone` holds both in the real game.
- **What lies loose and is light is heaved by hand** (`Loose::shove`, `matter::SHOVED`, 2026-11-13). With empty hands, the key that takes up a hunk (F, or a left click), on a log or a lump that lies at rest as itself and weighs 200 kg or less, heaves it a hand's breadth away; it comes to rest again as anything let go does, and the heave is an edit in the journal. What is heavier is broken up and carried. Walking into a thing does not shove it: that was tried first, and what is light enough to shove is low enough to step over, so nobody is ever stopped by it. Stone is 40 kg a cell: only the smallest lumps of rubble go, and thin logs. `make shatter` heaves a lump of a broken stone three times in the real game and makes the region again from its journal.
- **What comes down on another piece in the air rides on it** (`Loose::step`, 2026-11-13). Pieces met only the world, and each other once one had come to rest, so a hunk let go over a falling log went through it. Now each dropping piece is known by the cells it is in; one that comes down on another whose middle is under its own drops no faster than that one, and is on it when it lands. By the cell and by how fast each drops, no more: they do not bounce or turn each other, and a tree going over or a piece tipping meets nothing till it rests. (The developer, of what the physics must do: "how everything collides with each other".)
- **What comes to rest in somebody puts them out of it** (`Sim::step`, 2026-11-13). Pieces do not meet bodies in the air: they meet where a piece lands. It strikes whoever is under it (`matter::harm`), and whoever is then in its cells is moved to the side away from it where there is room within a metre, else up onto it. Before, a log bucked beside a person lay through his legs and held him.
- **The world is cells, and the only thing anything is stopped by.** A tree, a wall, a hunk put down is cells, so it is ground to whatever comes after it and nothing more has to be said about it. A new kind of thing in the world needs no collider of its own: it is cells, or it is one of the two below.
- **A body** (a creature, the player) is an upright box held out of solid cells. It walks up what is no higher than its step (0.55 m: a hunk, a thin log, a block of the ground) and must jump what is higher; that one number is what can be walked over. Bodies push each other apart over the ground.
- **A piece** is cells cut loose, one rigid thing until it comes to rest and is cells again. In the air it is stopped by the world alone: it does not meet bodies or other pieces until it has landed, when it is the world, and whoever was under it is struck.
- **One gravity** (22 m/s², more than twice the earth's, as a jump wants) for bodies and for pieces that drop. **A tall thing that goes over about its foot goes at the earth's** (9.81), no faster at its top than 13 m/s, and begins slowly as a tree hangs on its hinge: how long a tree takes to come down is what says how big it is. At the one gravity a birch was down 1.8 s after it was cut through, its top at 25 m/s; it now takes 3.5 s and comes at 13 (a willow 1.3 s then, 2.5 now).
- **What a thing weighs changes none of how fast it falls; it changes what it does.** Everything has a mass, from what it is made of (`matter::density`: wood 650 kg a cubic metre, stone 2,600, earth 1,500, leaf 40) and how much of it there is, and nothing has a weight written anywhere else. What lands on a body takes health by its mass and its speed (`matter::harm`): it had been by its speed and the cube root of how many cells it was, so a cubic metre of leaf struck as a cubic metre of stone. A tree that comes down strikes whoever is under any of its trunk, a metre to either side of the line from its foot to where its top struck (`Landed::along`): by its size about its top, it missed whoever stood under its middle. A landing is as loud as its mass and speed make it, and is a crash over 400 kg. A hunk of wood is 80 kg and is carried at a walk; one of stone is 325 and is hauled at six tenths of one (`matter::burden`).
- **What should keep its shape is not put into the grid** (`loose::Rested`, 2026-11-13). The developer: "one issue we have in the game right now things cant have angles ... it'd be nice if those logs could just lay at an angle ... we still wanna rely on voxels but forcing everything to be exact on the grid is not amazing visually", and of the game this one looks to, "has angles in some places and it is nice". A piece that comes to rest was put into the world by asking each cell of the world what of it was there: a log lying across the grid came out as steps, or was turned to lie along it. A piece may now keep itself (`Piece::keeps`): at rest it is still its own cells and the way it lies, and the game draws it so (`cube_game::loose::fall`); the world gets only the cells it is in, of a kind that is not drawn (`Block::whole`), so it stops a body, bears what is put on it and is loose again as itself when what is under it goes (`Loose::rouse`). This is how the game we look to holds everything; here it is for what is loose, and the land stays a grid. Logs are so. A felled tree is the same idea with its grown shape for its cells (`Thing`). Hunks put down by hand are in the grid on purpose: what is built with should line up. Rubble is so too ("things dont have to stick to the grid ... rubble or something could also just be (physics-wise) laying on top"): a lump of stone lies as it tumbled, and its stone is its own, in the world only as where it is (`Block::Scree`, to the bit where it is finer than a cell). A pick breaks a lump into hunks by its own record. Earth is not: it heaps. A piece that keeps its shape does not stop at its first touch: it tips about what it touched until its weight is inside what holds it (looked at sixteen ways round its middle: something it touches a hand that way, or none of it reaching further that way; a touch to each side was the first test, and a lump lay on one edge at 45 degrees), three times at most (`Piece::tips`), and a lump of stone on a heap rolls off it as loose rubble always did, so a lump lies on a face and against its neighbours, not on a corner. What it is in the world as is to the bit, 5 cm, not the cell: asked cell by cell, a log at an angle stood a body up to 30 cm over or in the wood drawn and a fifth of it bore nothing; to the bit it is 3 cm and a fiftieth (`cargo world lying`). The same trial walks a person across each log from both sides: never in it, never held on top of it, over a half-metre log on foot and any log with a jump. Before it rests it is put up out of what it came down into, 5 cm at a time until under a thirtieth of it is in anything (`loose::sunk`): pieces meet each other in the air only by the cell and a touch is felt at a few places, so 5 to 15% of a log's wood lay in the ground or the log beside it; now under 2%. A thing at rest is one body: it is let go only when half of it or more is held by nothing, and leaf gives way to it where it lies. Taken up, it takes its own bits out of each cell it is in and no other's (`Loose::unrest`): two logs against each other lie in the same cells, and all of a cell went with whichever was taken up first. (A log that came down in a bush could put only one 5 cm bit of itself where the leaf left room; that bit had nothing under it, the whole log was let go, fell where it lay, and came to rest, for ever: the piece the developer saw "infinitely falling on itself". One log of a great tree landed 370 times in half a minute.)
- **Not done, and where the next things go:** pieces meet each other in the air only by the cell and how fast each drops, and a body only where one lands (a tree strikes as it lands, not as it passes); nothing is pushed by walking into it (a light thing is heaved by hand, below). Whether a thing breaks goes by what it is made of and how fast it came, which is right: how much of it there is does not come into it.

### What is cut loose falls (`cube_sim::loose`, 2026-11-12)

The role model is Teardown: everything can be cut, and what a cut leaves standing on nothing comes down. Ours is an open world made from a seed, not a level, so it is done on the world's own cells with no physics engine.

- **A cut** (`Loose::cut`) digs a ball to the bit, then looks for what is no longer held: from each solid cell beside the cut through solid neighbours (faces, edges and corners: a thin limb climbs from cell to cell by their edges). More than `HELD` (30,000) cells, or `LAND` (4,000) cells of the land itself, and it is held. Fewer and closed: loose. Leaves bear nothing.
- **The land is there first, and what stands on it comes down to it** (2026-11-13; `Limb::feet`, `cargo world roots`). A trunk's shape is its own and knows no land: its roots were wood to a hand under its foot, and a root reaches a metre and more out, so on a bank the downhill one hung in the air, on three trees in five (the developer saw one: "one of the roots of the tree here is floating above the ground vs angling with it ... its probably more the renderer needs to be more self aware"). The shape now says which cubes are the lowest of its foot, and whoever knows the ground brings each down to it: the simulation by the cells the world has, the picture by the height the land gives, three metres at most. About one tree in twenty still has 20 to 45 cm of air under some wood at its foot (four seeds). Tried first and dropped: the trunk begun deeper and its roots made wood a metre down (three in five to two in five, and a tenth more faces); its foot set at the lowest ground its roots reach (to one in four). Both moved the tree to suit the land, where the answer was to let its roots know the land. 3% more faces of wood up close.
- **A tree or boulder is a drawn shape until it is cut into** (above: `flora`). The first bite makes all of it *wrought*: its blocks become `Timber`, `Brush`, `Shard` (`Block::wrought`), which the mesher draws as the cells and bits they are, and its limbs and leaf leave the region's shapes (`Cut::trees`; `flora::Grown::again` remakes the mesh, the old one staying until then). Which limbs are one tree is what `plants` noted as it grew them (`Limb::tree`): trees in a wood touch, and by cells alone one cannot be told from the next (a search through touching wood took half the wood; through faces alone it left limbs behind).
- **An untouched tree is scenery**: it bears nothing and stops nothing loose. Stopped by standing wood, nothing cut in a wood ever came down. Tried again 2026-11-13 with only the solid heart of a standing trunk stopping a fall (whole wood cells, not the bits of twigs and bark): of five trees felled (`cargo world fell`, five seeds) one came down right; the others hung for 30 s, lay with their middles 11 to 18 m up, or lost a quarter of their cells. A felled tree goes through standing ones on purpose, until a hung tree is something designed (slide off, or bring the other down). **And a tree that has been cut into holds only itself** (2026-11-13): where two such stand close, a cell is the one's whose limb it is nearest (`Thing.limbs`), the search for what holds a body does not pass from one tree's cells to the other's, and what falls goes through the other as through an untouched one. Before, a birch cut clean through hung by its twigs from a tree half a metre off that the same bite had made cells, and lay 14 m up in it when it was let fall (seed 1). `cargo world fell` on ten seeds; `make prove` tries three. Whose a cell is goes by how far it is outside each limb where the limb is as thick as it is there (`loose::outside`), not by the limb's thickest: by that a tree tapering from a metre to a hand's breadth owned everything within a metre of its top.
- **A tree first cut into is made cells to the bit** (`loose::work`, 2026-11-13). Low down its wood is written in the cubes it is drawn in (`plants::wood`), which reach cells its shape at a cell's size does not (a root's flare, the edge of a leaning trunk). Those stayed as they grew: wood nobody draws and nothing rests on (a dump of one birch's foot had 40 such cells). Near its foot the cells beside each cell are now made wrought too, where they hold that limb's own wood.
- **`cargo world fell` holds a felled tree to having gone over** (40 degrees from upright, from `Thing.lies`), not only to lying low: its middle under half its height passed a tree put back standing. `cargo world fell X,Z` fells the tree nearest a place. `cargo world joined` makes every tree near the start the cells it would be and says whether its wood is one body with its foot: on seed 6, 10 of 217 have a cell or two apart, and 58 cells of limbs are air in the world as it is made.
- **What breaks keeps its stuff** (`Loose::shatter`, `loose::land`, 2026-11-13). `cargo world quarry` picks at a boulder and digs the earth from round and under it until it comes down; a boulder that came down had a half to two thirds of its stone afterwards. Three things: what was too small to be a lump was dust (it goes with the largest lump beside it, or is a small stone of its own); a whole cell of stone or wood that found its place taken was dropped (it goes in the first room over it that has something bearing under it, and a bit the same); and a boulder is mostly its broken outside. And what comes to rest lies square: a lump, or a thing dropped straight, is turned to the nearest quarter turn each way and moved to whole cells as it lands (`loose::square`), since the world is cells and what lay turned between them lost its thin broken outside to being asked cell by cell what of it was there. 98 to 100% on seven seeds. A felled tree is not squared: it is long and would jump. And a lump that comes down on a heap rolls off it (`Loose::step`): where the ground beside it is lower than where it lies by more than its own height it goes that way and falls again, three times at the most; left where they met, one stone's lumps stood on one another two metres high. A boulder cut through stays where it lies on the bank, and one that lies on rock is not brought down by a shovel: both right.
- **What a felled tree does not put back is leaf** (`Landed.lost`, 2026-11-13): of the 23% one tree lost coming down in a thicket (seed 2), 1,811 cells were leaf into another tree's leaf, onto the ground or into standing wood, and 12 were wood. `cargo world fell` holds the wood to its own bar (under a hundredth of the piece) and the whole to seven in ten; ten seeds pass.
- **A broken cell is drawn by the ground at the foot of its column, however high it stands** (`mesh::Eased::stood`, 2026-11-13). The bits of a broken cell are drawn moved by how the eased ground is moved under them, so that what stands on the ground stands on it as drawn. That was asked at the cell's own height: for a trunk's cells, written to the bit for three metres up, it was by where the ground lay far under them, and all of a cut tree's foot was drawn in the wrong place (the mesher put 0 to 5 faces at each level of it where there are now 200). Seen as a break a hand wide through a leaning birch, from one side only. Found only by casting rays through the picture's own pixels at the break (they hit cells that were not drawn) and then counting the mesher's faces level by level; before that it was taken in turn for a stale mesh, a region made again, a neighbour's cells taken, unconverted bits, a slit in the trunk (which was real, and closed) and the level of detail. Looking at a felled tree from `--view fell` is what found it: no trial did.
- **A piece** is the loose cells taken out of the world and moved as one body: tall on a small foot it goes over about its foot the way it was pushed; anything else drops. When its wood or stone meets ground it is put back cell for cell where each now lies (every cell of the world it reaches is asked what of the piece is there, so no gaps however it is turned); what then rests on nothing falls in its turn; a crumb under four cells is gone.
- **Cost** (`cargo world fell`): a bite 1.5 ms; a 10,000-cell tree down in 3 s, 97% of its cells put back, the dearest step (the landing) 6 to 10 ms.
- **The game only draws** (`cube_game::loose`): each piece meshed by the world's own mesher from a world holding only the piece, so it looks falling as it did standing and will lying, and moved as the simulation moves it. A script's `chop` bites the nearest trunk.
- **Stone and earth that come down hard shatter** (`Loose::shatter`, over 4.5 m/s): into lumps some three cells across, thrown out and up and turning, each landing on its own as rubble. Wood does not. Nothing random: how a lump is thrown goes by where it was.
- **What comes down strikes whoever is under it** (`Sim::step`): harm by its speed and size, a tree a killing blow and a clod a bruise.
- **A thing is cut by the tool that is for it** (`Item::cuts`, `items::Stuff`; the developer, 2026-11-12: destruction is earned, a tree chopped and ground dug, not anything erased anywhere): a shovel takes earth out of its ball, an axe wood, a pick stone, and each leaves the rest (`loose::carve`). What is cut away is carried: timber, stone, earth, one for a cell's worth (`Sim::took`).
- **A tree an axe is at is drawn as the tree it grew as, until it is broken into logs** (2026-11-13; `Block::whole`, `Thing::form`, `cube_game::loose::felled`). The developer, having felled one: "when its chopped down even at the beginning it doesnt really look like the tree anymore", "the tree also needs to stay ... roughly the same shape as it was, which should roughly be straight, when it falls". At its first blow it had become cells a quarter of a metre a side, its fall broke its twigs off, and lying it was asked cell by cell where it was. Now its cells are a kind the ground's mesher does not draw (`Log`, `Bough` and the rest: cells in every other way, so it still bears, falls, stops a body and is cut), and the game draws its own shape: among the standing shapes until it is cut through, with a notch of pale cut wood drawn on it (`Thing::notch`); then alone, moved as the piece it is while it goes over and as it lies (`Thing::lies`); bare once its boughs are off; and as cells from the first blow that breaks its trunk (`Thing::cells`), which makes them cells that are drawn. Its stump is drawn as the tree's own foot where it stands (`Thing::stump`, `Thing::stumped`), until an axe is taken to it: made cells drawn as cells when the tree went, it was a heap of them beside a tree drawn as a tree. It comes down whole: nothing breaks from it as it strikes. What a carving cut makes of a tree (`Loose::cut`) is cells drawn as cells, as before.
- **A tool does to a thing what is next for that thing; it does not carve it** (the developer, 2026-11-13; `Loose::chop`). Only earth is dug freely, with the shovel. An axe's blow goes to the tree it strikes:
  1. *Standing*: into one notch a metre up its trunk on the side the blows come from, and the tree stands until it has taken as many as its girth asks (`BLOWS`: one to every 15 cm across, 2 to 8; the developer was not sure of the number). The last cuts a cell's height of trunk through and it falls.
  2. *Fallen*, it is still a tree: one blow knocks off all its boughs and leaf ("the first chop probably should cut away the branches"), which the game throws as chips that fade (`Loose.shed`).
  3. *Bare*, it is timber (`Loose::timber`): one blow bucks all of it into logs (every piece of it: a thick trunk lay in two or three, and took as many blows), one to every 3 m of its length (`LOG`: at 2 m a tree of 17 m was eight logs and 24 hunks). A log is a straight length of one thickness (one to three cells, by how much wood the trunk had), lying along the line the trunk lay on, at whatever angle that is (a piece that keeps its shape: `Rested`, below), with bark on its sides and cut wood at its two ends (`Block::Balk`, `Block::ends`). One break a blow took eight blows a tree, each a cell's width out of what was already cells, and could hardly be seen: "when it breaks it should kind of be more obvious, and the detail of it ... getting closer to the wood chunks should make it more refined". So each step is tidier than the last: the tree as it grew, its bare trunk, squared logs, cubes.
  4. *A log*: a blow splits it into hunks, two from a small log (one or two cells thick) and three from a large (three cells: a great tree's; `SPLITS`), whatever wood it has: "2 blocks for a small log and 3 for a large? shouldnt be an excessive amount by any means". (By all its wood a tree gave 26, "too many"; by half of it, 12 to 14.) A birch now gives 4, a tree of 17 m ten to twelve, and a great tree three a log. A scrap under a metre long is chips.
  Timber is found by what is there, not asked of the tree: the piece struck is all the cut wood joined to the cell the blow landed on, and its length is measured on it. Asked of the tree (where it lay when it fell, and which of its break points were cut), a log that dropped a metre when the trunk was broken was found by no blow, and half the wood of a thin tree could not be split; nothing is kept of a tree once it is bare.
  Each blow is one line of the journal (`Edit::Chop`), so it replays. A blow goes to a tree, not to a stump or a log lying beside it (one nearer the blow took every blow, and the tree stood). The cut that fells looks through the whole of the tree however large (`Loose.whole`): a great tree is more cells than `HELD` and was taken for the land, so that cut clean through it stood in the air, which the developer saw. `cargo world fell` felled one birch a seed and passed throughout; `make fells` fells the tree nearest each of 25 places, where 17 of 35 failed when it was first asked. A boulder goes the same way, and is already fallen (`Loose::chip`): a pick chips it, a little off at each blow, 3 to 5 blows by how far across it is (`CHIPS`), and at the last it breaks into hunks where it lay, from the ground up. The land's own stone the pick still digs. Loose stone (rubble: what a boulder or a cliff became when it came down) a blow breaks into hunks at once, the piece struck being all the loose stone joined to it (`Loose::rubble`, as timber); a scrap of under half a hunk goes in chips.
- **Resources are hunks** (the developer, 2026-11-13: "resources are effectively these chunks, that are kind of just larger voxels ... all of similar size ... you have to carry them in your hands, probably one at a time"; The Forest is the model, what is built with is hauled). A hunk is a cube two cells a side, half a metre (`loose::HUNK`, the one place the size is; the developer left it to us), of `Block::Billet` (wood) or `Block::Cobble` (stone): cells in the world like any other, so it is solid, drawn, stood on and built with, with nothing new to draw or collide. (Called a hunk in the code: a chunk is already 32 cells of the world.) Rules, each found by `cargo world hunks` failing:
  - *Nothing is kept of where hunks are* (`hunk_at`): a hunk is its eight cells. It lies on the squares that start at even cells, at whatever height it came to rest, counted in hunks from the lowest of its kind under it. So it replays from the journal like any edit (`Edit::Lift`, `Edit::Lay`).
  - *A hunk is its own body, held by what is under it and nothing beside it* (`Loose::loosen`): joined as cells are, one against another hung in the air. Each falls as itself, takes no leaf with it, comes down whole (up a cell at a time until all of it has room) and does not break or roll. No tool cuts one.
  - *Taken up and put down by hand* (`Loose::lift`, `Loose::lay`; F, or the left button with one in the hands): a person carries one (`Agent::carries`), walks while they do, and uses nothing from the pack. Wood and stone are no longer counted in the pack; earth still is.
  - *As much comes out as went in*: a log gives its wood in hunks to the nearest one, a scrap of under half a hunk gives chips. `cargo world hunks` passes on ten seeds.
  - Found on the way, and true of everything that falls: a boulder nobody has cut now stops and holds what comes down on it (things fell through it, and a hunk on one fell for ever); a whole cell landing in a part-filled one fills what room there is (a log that dropped a hand's width onto the broken ground under it lost two thirds of itself); cut wood does not roll.
  The building system and crafting are not built, by the developer's word: only the resources, so they need not be built again. The hunk is drawn on his hands, where they are drawn (`cube_game::loose::carried`), under a clip that holds his arms out (`carry`, [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#where-motion-comes-from)): put where the simulation has him, it rode at his head on a climb, which the walker takes a step behind. A pick's bite of the land's own stone that takes half a hunk's worth or more leaves a hunk on the side the blow came from (`Loose::cut`), by the bite alone, nothing kept between bites, so a region's edits replay on their own. A hunk of wood is faced with wood split along its grain (`Grain::Split`, a photograph like the land's others, `assets/textures`): the cut end is a photograph of a round log on black and is no face of a cube, and in bark it wore bark's moss. F is pressed by real keys in `make window` (`--carry` starts him with a hunk: put down, taken up).
- **A felled tree lies on its trunk** (2026-11-13). It is tried against the world only by its trunk and its thickest limbs (six tenths of its trunk's thickness and over); what is thinner gives way under it and is crushed where it goes into the ground. Felt for by every bough and twig, every felled tree lay propped on its own branches, 1.4 to 2.4 m up at its middle: the developer saw one lying level in the air and took it for a bush holding it. `cargo world fell` now measures the air under the trunk at its butt, middle and top (it had held only to its middle being under half its height, which passed all of them): 0 to 0.5 m on ten seeds, 1.1 on one that lies across a hump, which is right for a stiff trunk (`LOOSE_TRACE=1` says what stopped it each time).
- **A tree's fall is staged, and the same every time** (the developer, 2026-11-12: what is cut down should behave in a specific way, and predictably: it falls, and is then cut into logs; it does not burst into pieces, though a few small ones may fall from it): it goes the way it leans more than the way it was pushed; it hangs a moment on its stump and then goes faster and faster; it comes down whole, trunk and limbs and leaf, along the line it fell, and lies there to be cut up. Only the outermost of its crown (further from the trunk than a third of its height) snaps off as it strikes, and drops where it is. (First every limb broke off and was flung: ninety lumps a tree.) Then it settles (`Piece::settling`): its top is down and its butt is still up on the stump, so it slides on a stride and turns back about its top until its butt is on the ground beside the stump. (Stopped where its top struck, it lay as a ramp from stump to tip, and a length cut from its middle hung in the air.)
- **A trunk cut most of the way through snaps** (`loose::snap`): if fewer than half the trunk's fibres run unbroken from over the cut to under it, what is left at the thinnest level goes, a whole cell's height of it (what is joined is told by the cell: a slit one bit high parts nothing). Found with the real axe, 2026-11-13: its swing is aimed a little down, three chops bored a slanted tunnel, and the tree stood on the strips either side while every later chop went through the hole. Judged first by the least wood at any one level, a slanted tunnel never tripped it. `LOOSE_TRACE=1` prints each cut: what it took and what is loose after.
- **A fallen trunk is cut into logs by chopping across it** (the developer's choice, 2026-11-12, over splitting by itself): each cut through separates a length, which stays where it lies (`a_trunk_lying_on_the_ground_is_cut_into_lengths_that_stay_where_they_lie`).
- **What is cut is still a thing** (`loose::Thing`; the developer, the same day: a tree must be known as a tree still, since a world this size is made and dropped as it is walked and cannot keep every box). Each tree or boulder cut into is noted as the shape it grew (its limbs and leaf, a few hundred numbers), every bite taken out of it, and where it lies once it is down. Its cells can be made again from that.
- **The world is its seed and what was done to it** (`loose::Edit`, `Loose::journal`, `loose::replay`, 2026-11-13). Every cut and every thing put down is noted with its moment (how many steps of falling had been taken), 40 bytes each. Done again in order to the land as the seed makes it, they give the same world cell for cell, since nothing in a cut or a fall is random. `cargo world replay`: 23 edits (920 bytes: a tree felled and cut across, holes dug, earth put down) changed 20,502 cells and were done again in 43 ms with 0 cells different, on three seeds. This is what saving a world is to use, and what the game drops changed regions by:
- **A changed region is dropped like any other and made again from the seed and the journal** (`world::regions`, 2026-11-13). Changed regions that touch are one group (what fell in one may lie in the next): the group is dropped when all of it is far enough and nothing is falling, and made again as the seed has it; when every region of the group and the ring round it is there, the group's edits are done again in order, three milliseconds of it a frame (`loose::Replay::advance`; all at once it was a frame of 50 ms and more), and what is cells again leaves the shapes drawn. Until then it is seen as it was before anyone touched it (far off: a region is made at 208 m). `make forget` tries it in the real game (a script's `forget` drops every changed region at once): trees felled and cut up across one region and across four came back with 0 cells different.
- **For that the land had to be the same whatever order it is made in**, and it was not quite: young trees, bushes, logs, stumps and rocks were grown only by the region their foot was in and written whole, over its edge, so a region made again alone had lost what its neighbours had laid over it (136 cells of one). They are now grown by every region they reach and written only in that region's own columns, as trees are (`terrain::fill`, `plants::only`); whether one is crowded out by a tree is told from where the trees stand (`land.thing`), not from cells a neighbour does not have; a rock finds its ground from the land's height, not the cells.
- **Digging and putting back are tried the same way** (`make dig`, 2026-11-13): the shovel's holes, the earth carried, each lump put down where he looks, then `forget`. A lump put down is one cell's worth (0.155 m across the ball), as one carried is: at 0.18 it was a cell and a half and ground dug and put back came to more than was dug.
- **Earth put down falls and heaps; stone and timber stay** (`loose::heaped`, 2026-11-13): a lump of earth comes to rest on what is under it and runs down any side that falls away by more than 0.45 m, a lump's width at a time (twenty let go at one place make a heap three cells high at most). Worked out from the cells in a fixed order, so a replay rests each lump in the same place. Stone and timber stay where they are put: that is building.
- **What is put where nothing holds it falls** (`Loose::put`, 2026-11-13): stone or timber put down is looked at as a cut is, and with nothing under it comes down, and breaks if it comes down hard. Before, a stone let go in the air hung there. `make shatter` tries it in the real game: a stone 1.5 m across from 5 m lands at 13.6 m/s, is rubble in some twenty lumps, is heard, and the region made again from seed and journal is the same (the script's `drop` is a put, so it is in the journal).
- **Building with what was cut is tried in the real game** (`make build`): a tree felled, limbed, broken and split, four hunks taken up one at a time and put down, each on the last; held to what was under each as it was put, and the region made again is the same. (Held only to four taken up and four put down, it passed while the script took the same hunk up and down four times: the picture showed one hunk on the ground.) `Loose::put` still puts stone or timber down as a whole cell, for tests and the stone a script lets go; a wall so built (eight cells by six, in a test) stands, and comes down when its foot is cut away.
- **Not yet**: the journal is not written to disk; the first cut into a great tree is one long step (235,000 cells made wrought in 30 ms in the worst seen; it was 53 ms while the cells were gathered into a set first).
- **A cut lands with the blow** (`Sim::use_held`, `player::drive`): a tool is swung as anything is, one blow at a time, and what it cuts is cut `SWING` (0.35 s) later.
- **It is heard and seen** (`cube_game::loose::told`): the simulation notes each cut, each thing come loose and each landing (`Loose::cuts`, `begun`, `heard`); the game plays an axe, a spade or a pick, a tree's crack, a crash or a clatter (at most one clatter in an eighth of a second: a crown that breaks is ninety limbs landing at once), and throws up chips of what was cut. The sounds of wood and stone are recordings (BigSoundBank, CC0; `tools/audio/cut.sh` has how each is cut): made of noise with ffmpeg they were poor (the developer: "the noises suck"). A blow into a tree is the knock of an axe on a log; the blow that parts something (the last that fells, limbs knocked off, a trunk into logs, a log into hunks) is the bright crack of an axe on dry wood, so that a break is heard as one ("when it breaks it should kind of be more obvious"); a tree going is twigs breaking at half speed; only a tree's landing is the crash, wood else lands with a knock (`Landed::wood`) and stone with a stone let fall. A spade is a shovel dug into the ground; a pick is a shovel struck, iron on what is hard (no pick on rock was found).
- **What is carried is put down as what it was** (`Item::places`, `Used::Place`, `Sim::placed`): earth in hand goes into the air where he looks, a lump for each one carried; a hunk in the hands goes down whole (`Used::Lay`, `Sim::laid`).
- **Water runs into what is opened to it** (`loose::flow`, after every cut): from water beside the cut, sideways, down, and up no higher than it stood, to 6,000 cells. A hole dug at a lake's edge fills to the lake's level. By the cell; a lake is not drained by it.
- **Stone that comes loose is rubble** (`Block::Shard`): the land's own stone is drawn eased into the ground round it and takes its colour, and a lump of it lying in a meadow was drawn half grass.
- A script's `chop` bites the nearest standing trunk, and once that is down, across the trunk where it lies (looked at 2026-11-13: a clean gap, both lengths left lying). A script's `drop` lets a block of stone go five metres up, to see it (looked at 2026-11-12: it breaks, piles, and a second breaks on the pile).
- **A piece's mesh is made on another thread** (`cube_game::loose::fall`) and drawn a frame or two on: made on the frame's thread a tree coming loose was a frame of 40 to 56 ms; now the worst is about 30.
- **Not yet**: a falling tree goes through standing ones; water is by the cell and does not run out of what it left.

### What grows is drawn as shapes

- **`plants` says what shape it grew** (`plants::recorded`, `Form`): as it sets the cells of a trunk, a limb or a mass of leaf, it notes the limb (from, to, how thick at each end, what bark) or the clump (middle, reach, what leaf). The game records this as it makes each region (`world::regions`), keeps the shapes whose middle lies in the region (`flora::Forms`), and makes one mesh a region from them (`flora::grow`): a seven-sided tapering tube a limb, and for a clump some tens to two hundred small cards over an ellipsoid, each a piece of the leaf picture with its gaps, lit as the mass and not as a card, free to the wind.
- **The mesher does not draw wood or leaf cells** when that is on (`mesh::MODELLED`), nor let them hide the ground under them. They are still cells: bodies stop at trunks, creatures do not climb crowns.
- **Cost**: frames went from 11 ms to 8 in the meadow; cube crowns were more faces than the cards that replaced them.
- **Smooth ground is one surface** (`mesh::Heights`): where each column's ground ends is found from the cells, the surface at a corner is the mean of the sixteen columns round it, and a quad is drawn a column with its corners on that surface and its colour the mean of what tops those columns; steep, it shows earth and then stone. No risers are drawn at all. The first smooth ground eased each cube's own corners, which left a stretched riser on every steep face (sawteeth up a cliff) and a zigzag wherever two materials met. The far levels draw the same surface a quad to two or four columns.
- **The ground was smooth by default for a few hours** (`Ground::Smooth`, now `--smooth`; the default is blocks again, see below), now that nothing natural beside it is cubes; and the water's surface runs two cells on under the bank, so a shoreline is where smooth ground meets it and not a sawn edge.
- **What grows is cubes by default** (`flora::Shape::limb_cubes`, `clump_cubes`, `stone_cubes`, 2026-11-11; `--shapes` for the low-poly forms). Made from the same `plants::Form`, at three levels:
  - Near (a region within 5 m): trunks and heavy limbs in cubes of 10 cm, round to the cube, swelling into stepped roots; a lesser limb is one line of cubes as thick as it is (a hundred pine boughs drawn round were more faces than the rest of the wood, unseen under their needles). Each cube its own shade of bark and a line of them along the grain shares one, so furrows; moss on roots and foot; cut wood on an end nothing grows from. Leaf is boxes from a hand to 60 cm, each its own green, lit on top and dark under, over a dark heart. Stone is cubes in tipped beds of differing shade, moss and lichen on top.
  - To 46 m: the world's 25 cm cells for wood thicker than 20 cm, larger and fewer leaf boxes, a conifer's spray one plate.
  - Beyond: 50 cm, the largest masses only, and no shadow cast.
  - Cost: the first try was 4.2 million faces in a forest view and 27 ms. With the levels above it is 1.7 million and 16 ms; meadow 13 ms. A flora mesh carries `world::Faces`, so a picture's `pace` counts it.
- **So do the ground's blocks** (`mesh_chunk`, the stepped path): a top is darkened at a corner by how many of the three columns round it stand over it by a hand or more, so the foot of every bank and terrace is in shade.
- **Cubes shade their own corners** (`flora::Shape::cubes`): a corner of a face is darkened by how many cubes stand over it beside that face, the occlusion every good cube renderer has (research, 1). Baked into the corner's colour, so it costs nothing to draw and reaches farther than screen-space occlusion's few texels.
- **A limb has one shape, and it is the simulation's** (`plants::Limb::fill`, 2026-11-11): round, tapering, wandering, ridged, a rooted trunk swelling into buttresses, in cubes of any size on the world's grid. The simulation fills cells from it (25 cm) and the game draws it finer (10 and 15 cm), so what stops a body is what is seen, to a cell. Before, cells were a plain cone with root sticks and the picture had the swell and the bends: the developer walked into wood he could see and was stopped by wood he could not. Not yet to the bit: a body is stopped by the cell, the eye sees the 10 cm cube.
- **Leaf does not stop a body** (`body`): a person pushes through a crown or a bush and is stopped by wood.
- **A leaf mass is a lump with its boxes on it** (`flora::Shape::clump_cubes`): a body of four crossed boxes, as near an egg as boxes come, and the smaller boxes sitting on its surface. And every limb that carries leaf is drawn at every distance, as one line of cubes if need be (only a conifer's drooping boughs, hidden under their needles, are dropped). The developer's pictures showed why: boxes hung in the air round a slab, and a young tree by the river was a crown with no tree under it.
- **Seen farther** (the developer: "draw distance is really poor"): real ground and trees are made to 208 m (176), the mist is half as thick, and there is a fourth level of detail for both (ground in 2 m blocks past 168 m; a tree past 104 m is its trunk and one box to a mass). Measured on a quiet machine it was 19 ms in a wood, and the range was not the cost (176 m measured the same). `FLORA_TRACE=1` prints each region's faces by level and part as it is made, and added up it was wood, not leaf: an oak's great limbs, a metre through, in 10 cm cubes high overhead (half a million faces in the one nearest region), and far trunks' stair-stepped cubes (half a million over the view). So wood is cut fine only within reach of the ground (4.5 m near, 6 m in the next ring) and in cubes twice the size above, and a far trunk is one box. Meadow 13 ms, wood 15, river 16, from the air 12. Two guesses made before measuring (leaf boxes, then mid-range limbs) changed nothing.
- **Cube trees and rocks are drawn with the whole paint** (`Look::whole`: nothing cut out, outsides only). The cut-out material the ground cover needs is far dearer where large faces lie over one another, as a crown's boxes do: the same forest view was 19 ms with it and 17 without, at the same faces. With the trees raised (`scale`: oak to 30 m, pine to 46, giants a quarter over) a great trunk is cubes of 15 cm and leaf boxes are capped by level; forest 17 ms, meadow 13.
- **Grass is slats; ferns and flowers are bits** (`mesh::blade`; `--bits` for grass as columns of bits, `--blades` for the pointed planes). A slat is a blade in the world's own shapes: square-cut, upright, lying along the grid one way or the other, a bit wide for most of its height and half that above, so a stepped outline and no point. Two faces. Columns of bits were tried first (five faces each, so half as many of them): true to the style, chunky underfoot, and the developer's word was that grass "can still be done efficiently, it just needs to use the right art style". A fern is a short crown and arcs of bits stepping out, up and down; a flower one bit of colour on a stem.
- **The far stand-ins are boxes too** (`far::Shape::crown`, `cone`): three boxes to a broadleaf, four square tiers to a conifer.
- **Limbs are wood, not pipes** (`flora::Shape::limb`): rings along their length that wander a little, ridged along the grain, an end rounded over where another limb goes on and cut wood (end grain) where none does. `plants::bole` notes how many roots a trunk has and the trunk is drawn swelling into them as one piece.
- **Bark is its true size along a limb** (`flora::Shape::limb`): each stretch between two rings takes its own piece of the tile, every ring but the ends laid twice. One tile pulled the length of a pine was bark drawn out five times too long, which read as a plain brown pole.
- **Short grass over a meadow is drawn, not held** (`mesh_chunk`, near level only): most cells of grass with nothing taller on them carry four to six short blades, thick in some patches and thin in others, tinted dry or lush as the ground under them is. The ground was a painted floor with tufts stood on it. A quarter of a million more faces in a meadow view; the frame stayed 13 ms.
- **Three levels of detail for what grows** (`flora::grow`, by a region's distance: 36 m, 88 m), as the ground has: sprigs, cards, a few broad cards. Sprigs everywhere cost 4 ms a frame; with the levels a meadow frame is 13 ms with the voxel ground.
- **Not done**: beyond the made regions the far land still stands rounded stand-ins for trees; rocks, flowers and mushrooms are still little cubes.

### The ground's blocks

- **The land is drawn in blocks of the size its slope calls for** (`mesh::Eased::step`, 2026-11-11; `mesh::Ground::Stepped`, the default). Nearly level ground is cut in steps of a bit (5 cm), so a meadow rolls; a slope is whole cells; steep ground is blocks half a metre a side and a rock face blocks of a metre. The land gets blockier as it gets wilder, and no two hillsides are the same staircase, which is what every slope was when each was drawn as its 0.25 m cubes. It is voxels throughout: only the size varies.
- **The cells are untouched.** Digging, building and what a body stands on are the same 0.25 m cells and bits; this is how the top cell of each column of land (`Block::ground`: soil, sand, snow, stone) is drawn. Its height is the mean height of the ground in the sixteen columns round it, rounded to the block's size, with a riser down to each neighbour that stands lower. Ground cover, the bits of a broken cell and a creature's feet (`mesh::eased_under`) are drawn at that height.
- **Stone that is not land is drawn as its cubes**: a column whose top would be drawn more than a few cells from where it is (a block standing alone, something built) falls back to plain cubes.
- **A low riser is lit and coloured as the ground it is a fold of.** Lit as a wall it took the warm light of the horizon while the ground beside it took the blue of the sky overhead, and a forest floor was a grid of orange lines.
- **Tried and set aside: the ground eased fully smooth** (`--smooth`). It was built first, from a misreading of what was asked. Smooth land under cube trees was two games in one picture. `--cubes` draws the old plain cubes.
- **Block size goes by place as well as slope** (`mesh::rough`): the land has rough country some twenty metres over, where a modest slope is already half-metre and metre blocks, and smooth country cut fine. By slope alone everything in one view was one size, and the developer saw only uniform voxels.
- **The far levels of detail draw the same ground** (`Eased::far_top`): each group of cells takes the eased height at its middle, in blocks its own size where the land is steep. Drawn as plain cubes of their own size the far levels were a different hill, striped in half-metre stairs from 40 m out.
- **Not done**: a cube-drawn neighbour of a stepped column can leave a gap; thin gaps show where two levels of detail meet on steep rock.

- **Blocks are the default again** (2026-11-11, the developer's word: voxels were never the trouble). `cargo world survey` counts faces as the game draws them, trees as shapes.

### Light

- **The sky is air scattering the sun** (Bevy's `Atmosphere`, 2026-11-11), so the colour of the sunlight, of the sky and of the light that fills shadows all come from where the sun is, and will follow it when there is a time of day. Before, a white sun and a flat blue fill lit everything evenly and nothing had depth.
- **The sun stands at 35 degrees**: everything throws a shadow longer than itself.
- **On every camera that looks at the world** (`world::look`): exposure for full sun, a filmic roll-off (TonyMcMapface), a little more saturation and contrast, bloom, screen-space ambient occlusion for the depth between one thing and another, and haze that warms toward the sun.
- **Tried and backed out the same hour**: lit air (volumetric fog) over the whole island turned the sky purple and the grass yellow; a stronger grade made shade black. Light shafts want a volume placed where they are wanted (a wood, a morning), not one over everything.
- **The calendar is the simulation's** (`cube_sim::sky`, 2026-11-11): one number, `when`, days since the world began with the hour as its fraction. A day is 24 minutes (a minute to the hour), seven days a season, four seasons a year from spring; the world starts mid-spring at 15:00. The sun rises in the east, stands to the south at noon, and swings with the year: summer noons 81 degrees up and days 13.5 hours, winter 43 degrees and 10.5. The moon goes through its phases once a season: it trails the sun by as much as it is through them, so a full moon rises at sunset and a new moon is not in the night sky at all; `moonlight` is how full it is (steeply: a half moon gives a third of a full one) and nothing when it is down. `--day N --hour H` on any scene.
- **The moon is drawn as what it is** (`air::heavens`): a ball with one bright half, kept turned to the sun, so its phase is seen by the real geometry. `--view moon --day N --hour H` looks straight at it. (The first try slid a dark disc over a bright one; a close picture showed a black ball beside the moon.)
- **Seasons are drawn by the wind shader** (`world::season_look`, a uniform on the land's material): leaves and grass take the season's tint by how much of a leaf each corner is (the number that already says how free it is to move), and in winter snow lies on whatever faces up. No block changes: the simulation's world is the same in every season. Not done: bare trees in winter, ice, anything living that notices the season.
- **The sky is drawn from the hour** (`world::sky`): the sun goes round and under and is put out below the horizon (the air does the reddening and dimming above it); a moon comes up opposite, bluer and brighter than a real one; the camera's exposure follows the sun's height, opening fourteen stops by night as an eye does, because a night as dark as night cannot be played in; colour drains under the moon; the haze warms at a low sun and nearly goes at night. The exposure for each height was set by eye against pictures (`--hour H` on any scene).
- **Twilight is the air's own, and the hour is exposed for the sky** (2026-11-12, after `make sky` showed dusk black and every sunset cream). The sun's light was put out by hand as it crossed the horizon, which also put out the sky: Bevy's air is lit only by that light. It is now left on until the sun is fifteen degrees under: the engine's shading already takes it off the land where the world is in the way, and the air over the horizon goes on scattering it, so there is an afterglow, a red rim under a blue hour, and the mountain is lit after the meadow is not. The exposure is a table by the sun's height (`world::EXPOSURE`) set for the sky as well as the land: with the sun on the horizon the sky round it is far brighter than anything it lights, and an exposure for the land alone (two and a half stops more open than now) left it white. (A table meant to give the land more sky light at a low sun, `SKYLIGHT`, was set on `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight`, whose strength the engine reads once when it is put on the camera; it never did anything, and the look that was judged and kept is without it. It is gone, and the strength is now set on `EnvironmentMapLight`, which the renderer reads.) The grade keeps its colour until night proper (it was drained from six degrees under, the hour with the most colour) and cools the twilight, which the air alone leaves brown. The haze's glare toward the sun goes out with the sun: left on, it lit the mist from under the ground all night. The sun's disc is drawn twice its true width.
- **Stars are squares of their own light, one mesh** (`air::setup`, 2026-11-12): 2,600 where there were 420, most faint and a few bright, white, blue and amber, two in five in a band across the sky (the Milky Way). They are added to the sky behind them and fade in from the end of twilight; as black balls with a light of their own they were black specks on every dawn and dusk. They are fainter toward the horizon and under a full moon, and the band has a faint glow of its own. They and the moon stand 12 km off, beyond the clouds.
- **What the sky is given that the air does not give it** (`cube_game::vault`, 2026-11-13, each from a fault a reviewer found in `make sky`): plain shapes round the camera with a colour of their own, laid on the sky behind everything. *A floor under the night*: with no moon the air is lit by nothing and the sky was black, darker than the clouds and land under it; it is deep blue, lighter at its edge, and the far land's haze goes into that colour. *Blue overhead while the sun is low*: exposed for the land, the air overhead at sunrise and sunset is a dead grey; it is blended toward blue, most overhead and not at all at the horizon. *A glow round the sun*, which the air's picture of the sky is too coarse to hold, and *a ring round the moon*. *The low sun itself*: the engine's disc has no red in it at any brightness (dimmed forty thousand times it was the sky's own tone and gone; a thousand times, a white hole), so under a quarter of the way up it is not drawn and a disc of ours is, in the sun's colour at that height, larger the lower it stands, and several times the picture's white (deepened and only twice white, the tonemapper made it a dull disc darker than the sky round it). The glow round the sun is half white: its own red laid on a sky already as red as a picture can be changed nothing. These are not lit and the exposure does not touch them: their numbers are the picture's.
- **The haze is the colour of the sky's edge at every hour** (`world::sky`, 2026-11-13). It is what the far land and the far sea go into, so where it was not the sky's colour the sea was a bar under the sky: darker than the sky at dawn and dusk (it was dimmed with the daylight, but the sky's edge stays bright in the picture all through twilight, since the exposure follows the light down), and darker than a moonlit sky. Away from the sun it is the day's pale blue, mauve at a low sun; toward the sun warm, and at a low sun the sky's orange, spread wide; at night the night's blue, lighter under a moon, and a little under the sky's edge, since the far mountain goes into it too. (Its numbers are the picture's, as `Color::srgb` takes them: the night's were first written as light, a twelfth as bright, and the bar stayed. A seventh of its warmth toward the sun stays on a while after sunset, since the sky on that side is still orange and the sea under it must be; all of it left on, the land glowed tan toward a sun that had set.) The glare toward the sun is the sun's light times a number, and the exposure opens three stops for a low sun: it is held to what it is at noon, or it grows into a custard that outshines the sky.
- **The haze toward the sun is set by how bright it is in the picture, not by the sun** (`world::sky`, 2026-11-13, after the same picture: the crowns across a lake were one flat tan, brighter than the sky). Bevy's fog adds the sun's light times the exposure toward the sun, untouched by the air that reddens and dims a low sun everywhere else; at sunset that came to ten times white in a wide lobe, and the fifth of it that sixty metres of air takes covered anything across a lake. The strength is now divided by the sun's light as exposed, to give three times white at noon (a narrow lobe) and 1.2 at a low sun. `make sky`'s `lake` is the view to judge it by.
- **Grass is seen from both sides and lit from one** (`double_sided: false` on the ground's material, with nothing culled; 2026-11-13). A blade's facing is up, as the ground's; lit as two-sided, a blade seen from behind had it turned down and was lit from under: black beside a torch, and one dark blade in two in a meadow by day.
- **The sea goes on to the horizon** (2026-11-13): plain sheets from where the waved ones end (1.5 km) out to 40 km. From anywhere high the engine's own ground showed past the sheets' edge as a tan slab under the sky, with the far land's hazed trees standing against it as pale squares. Out there everything is the haze's colour, so sea, far land and the foot of the sky meet in one tone. (Tried first: making the engine's ground as dark as sea. Under a bright dawn it was a black bar; sea at that distance mirrors the sky's edge and is as light.)
- **Mood is made, not found** (2026-11-11, after the first day and night were correct and flat):
  - *Cloud shadows.* A tile of soft patches made in code is laid in the sun's light (`DirectionalLightTexture`, Bevy's `pbr_light_textures` feature) and drifted downwind, so hillsides go dim and bright again. The light's own size sets the tile's (420 m); shadow cascades use only its rotation, so they are not disturbed.
  - *Mist.* A layer of thin lit air 24 m deep lies over the low ground (`FogVolume`, `VolumetricFog` on the camera, `VolumetricLight` on the sun): shafts of sun between trunks, far woods in haze. Kept low so the sky stays clear, and thinned and tinted with the sun as it drops, or a low sun floods it and whites the picture out. (Over the whole island and untinted it turned the sky purple.)
  - *A low sun is made stronger and amber.* True to life the last hour of sun is weak and the sky's flat fill takes the picture; the gold of evening is wanted more than the truth. Exposure near the horizon is held down so the sky keeps its colour.
  - *Night has a sky.* Stars on a dome round the camera, a moon where its light comes from, and one mote in six a firefly that swells and fades.
- **Mountains stand to the north** (`terrain::column`). The sun goes round by the south, so a mountain there is lit all day and never lies between the sun and the lowland. Placed anywhere, seed 6 had it due west and the start was in its shadow from mid afternoon: an hour was lost looking for a fault in the cloud shadows before a picture at another hour showed it.
- **Clouds are volumes of cubes, lit as volumes** (`cube_game::clouds`, `assets/shaders/cloud.wesl`, 2026-11-12). Each is a field of density (soft puffs heaped over a level foot) cut into cells, and a cell with cloud in it is a cube: 10 m ones on steps of 20 and 40 m, by the density at the middle of each size of block, so there are many sizes of cube and not one. What makes that a cloud and not a white rock is the light:
  - *Each cube faces the way the density falls off there, not the way its faces do* (its corners' normals), leaning a little to each face so a cube still reads as one. So one side of a cloud is bright and the other in shade whatever its faces are, and the engine's own sun, air and sky light it with nothing of ours: white over blue-grey at noon, amber at sunset, dim under the moon.
  - *The more cloud over a cube, the greyer it is* (in its colour): the dark level foot of a cumulus.
  - *The sun's light inside it* is added by a fragment shader, which is why the material is drawn forward: some from every side (less under the thickest of it), and more at an edge with the sun behind, the silver lining. An edge is a cube whose facing is square to the eye, which only the volume's facing can say. The game gives the shader the sun's colour at that height by a sum of how much air is in the way (once overhead, thirty-eight times at the horizon), so the clouds go orange and then, for a few minutes after the land has lost the sun, deep rose.
  - *Tried first and dropped:* boxes heaped by hand and lit by their own faces (three brightnesses, a pile of crates); and the light within as one flat glow on the engine's own material, which made a backlit cloud a paper cut-out.
  - *A low sun's light is thin and from one side* (after the same review: at sunrise and sunset every cloud was one custard colour all over, and then one amber): the light within is a quarter of what it is by day, and the side away from the sun is given the sky's blue, so a cloud is amber on one side and mauve on the other; under the horizon the sun's light is all a cloud has and is let show, from below, which is the rose of dusk.
  - *By day a cloud is white over a blue-grey foot*: as much of the sun's light comes out of its top as falls on it and little out from under its thickest part, where what does come out is bluer for the way it has come, and the deeper a cube the bluer its own white. (The tonemapper holds everything in the game under about 222 of 255: a cloud is the whitest thing there is and still not white.) (With less within and more under, they were plaster: never white, with cream feet.)
  - *A cube is still a cube in flat light*: each face carries a little of its own shade (top, sides, underside) and each cube its own white, since from under a cloud its smooth volume shading alone read as a blurred picture beside a land of crisp cubes.
  - *Shape*: puffs lie along a bent band, not over a round patch (from under, clouds were discs); some turrets stand well up; the edge is frayed by the single cube. How thick a patch is counts squared, so most of the sky is thin and a few places are a bank of clouds half as big again. Small clouds stand in clumps of up to three strewn well beyond their own place (one to a place was polka dots to the horizon), and one small one in eleven is of a higher, thinner kind at twice the height, drawn out along the wind and standing in rows of three or four (one alone was a plank).
  - Some 1,100 stand in a field 14 km across that drifts downwind and wraps round the camera (an eighth of them are moved each frame, which at their distance is not seen; with and without them `make pace` reads the same to within its own run-to-run difference, 7.3 to 7.7 ms): sixteen shapes, in fine cubes within 2.6 km and coarse beyond, turned one of four ways. Patches of the field are thick (banks), middling or thin, so the sky changes as it comes over; the world starts under a fair sky with a bank standing off to the south-east. Their feet are level at 430 m overhead and come down toward the field's edge, where each dwindles to nothing. They cast no shadow: the shade on the land is still the sun's own tile, drifting the same way, and does not lie under any one cloud. Not done: rain, a sky that is overcast, clouds the mountain stands in.
- **Not done**: weather, and torches that can be placed. (Creatures keep hours: [What lives in it](#what-lives-in-it).)
- **The torch** (`player::torch`, 2026-11-11): a warm point light with shadows and a waver; T lights it and puts it out, `--torch` starts with it lit. It costs about 4 ms a frame here (a shadowed point light draws the land six more times); not yet looked into.
- **The torch's light stands over the flame, not in it** (2026-11-12, after `make sky` showed the bearer a white figure in a straw-coloured pool full of black spikes). In the flame, a hand from his face and a man's height from the ground, the light gave his face thirty times what it gave the ground at his feet, by the square of how far, and every blade of grass threw a shadow many times its length. It is now 1.3 m over the flame: ground and bearer are lit alike and shadows are short. A tenth of it throws no shadow (a fire's light comes off everything it falls on; without that a shadow was a black hole), and a very small light in the flame itself is for his hand, the side of his face and the torch's head. The night's grade takes colour out for moonlight; while a torch is lit it is left in, or amber on grass is straw. *The hot middle of the pool* is a paler light in a cone from the same place, thrown down ahead of him where the torch is held out, so it misses him and the pool goes from pale and bright to deep amber: one light alone was one mustard hue from his feet to the dark. (The cone is nearly the fire's own colour and pale by being bright: of a yellower light it was khaki on grass.) *What comes back off the ground toward the eye* is a small light with no shadow between him and the camera: the near side of a trunk between the flame and the eye faces no part of the fire and was black. (Tried first as a warm fill over the whole night: the far woods went olive and there was no blue left for the fire to be warm against.) The flame is an orange body under a redder tip (as a pale one it was a white box with a red halo over an amber pool: three hues), with more glow round it by night (the camera's bloom is more in the dark). Not done: grass seen against the flame is black (a blade is thin and should glow lit from behind); a flame that moves; torches that can be placed.
- **A flame is given back three quarters of what the eye has shut** (`player::SEEN`, `world::shut`, 2026-11-13, after a player's picture of a lit torch at sunset that lit nothing, not the hand holding it). The exposure is ten stops shut at sunset from the night's, and a torch of its true strength shows only in night proper. Its lights are multiplied by two to the power of 0.75 times those stops, and by day by no more than at sunset: so it warms its bearer and the ground at his feet from sunset on, and at noon is a flame and no light. `make sky`'s `torch-shore` is a torch at dusk on a shore.
- **Under a roof of rock the eye opens and the sky's fill goes** (`world::Roofed`, 2026-11-14, after a cave dug to look at was as bright inside as the hill outside). Bevy lights everything from the whole sky wherever it is. Thirteen lines from the eye up into the sky are asked whether land (not a tree) stops them within forty metres; past two thirds hidden, over a second or two, the exposure opens toward `CAVE_EV` (10.5, four stops from noon's) and the sky's fill and the night's ambient fall to a fortieth as the picture shows them. It is by the eye, not by the place: from outside, the inside of a cave is still lit as the open is, and from inside, the land seen through the mouth has lost its fill (it is blown white by the exposure anyway). The torch is given its stops back from the exposure there is (`world::shut`), so it lights a cave at noon as it lights a night. A pool under the land takes shadows, where open water does not: the sun came through the rock onto it.
- **A cave is a set, not yet land** (`cube_game::cave`, `--cave`, `make cave`). The land is a height map, kept as cells a few metres deep and drawn as column tops with risers, so a cave is dug by the journal's own cuts into the nearest hillside with no tree in the way, after the stone under it is filled in; and every column over it is made the stone a broken boulder is, which is drawn as plain cells, because a column of ground beside one with air under it is drawn with a riser hanging six metres down, across the passage. The hill over the cave is therefore bare rock. Its shaft is cut along the line of the sun at whichever hour has least rock to go through and open sky beyond (it prints the hour). What the sky gives through the mouth and the shaft is two shadowed lamps outside them, an eighth as strong to an eye outside; what a patch of sun on the floor gives back is a shadowless light there while the sun is on the shaft's line. Not done: a beam seen in the air (the mist does not show one), light from the mouth as seen from outside (the passage is still too bright from the hillside), and real caves in the land, which need the mesher to draw roofs.

### Wind and the air

- **The season is applied in both wind shaders** (`wind.wesl`, `wind_prepass.wesl`): the second is also what the deferred path draws colour with, and for a day after water began to reflect the land (deferred by default) it left the season out, so autumn and winter were summer and no check knew. `make seasons` (in `make prove`) pictures the meadow in summer and winter and judges the ground's colour: green, then no greener than blue.

- **Pollen thins to nothing within three metres of the camera** (`air::motes`): a glowing speck a hand from the lens bloomed into a white ball the size of a head in every daytime picture.

- **What grows moves in the wind** (2026-11-11). The mesher says how free each corner is to move, in the alpha of its colour: ground and wood are held, a leaf gives two fifths, a blade of grass is held at its foot and free at its tip. Two vertex shaders push each corner downwind by that much (`assets/shaders/wind.wesl` for the picture, `wind_prepass.wesl` for depth and shadows, so a leaf's shadow sways with it): long gusts rolling downwind with a quick flutter under them. The push depends only on where a corner is and the time, so corners that meet move together and nothing tears. The land's material is Bevy's own with those two shaders swapped in (`world::Windy`); nothing else about how it is lit changes.
- **The two shaders hold the same push written twice.** The prepass keeps the time in a different binding from the forward pass, and a shared file imported by both was not tried. Change both together.
- **The pass that steadies the picture is told where the wind had each leaf a frame ago** (`wind_prepass.wesl`, 2026-11-12). It was told a leaf had not moved when the wind had moved it, so everything that sways was smeared and settled again every frame: the developer saw the textures of bushes "redraw non stop". Any push added to the picture's shader must be added there twice, for now and for the frame before.
- **Leaf slows a body to half its speed** (`Body::step`): a bush gives way, and was run through as if it were not there.
- **One wind** (`cube_sim::sky::wind(when)`, 2026-11-11): how hard it blows, 0 to 1, from the game's clock: weather over hours and days (still mornings, windy weeks) with gusts of some seconds on it. The game holds it as `world::Breeze` and everything takes it from there: the shaders' push (a uniform beside the season's), the wind's bed and the rustle of leaves, how fast pollen and falling leaves drift and when a crown lets a flurry go. Before, each of the three made its own gusts from the frame clock, so leaves were heard to gust while they hung still, and every day blew alike. `--wind 0.8` sets it; `make wind` (in `make prove`) pictures a wood half a second apart in still air and in a hard wind and judges how much each picture changed.
- **A whole tree leans with the wind** (2026-11-11): each corner of what grows carries how high it stands over the ground under it (the mesh's second texture place, `flora::Shape::high`; nothing for a stone), and both shaders push it downwind by that height squared, 40 cm at 14 m in the hardest wind, on a slower, longer swell than the leaves' stir. Before, leaves fluttered on trunks and boughs that never moved. By height over the ground and not over the tree's own foot, since a limb does not know its tree: it depends only on where a corner is, so nothing tears. At head height a trunk moves under a centimetre, so what stops a body is still the wood that is seen.
- **Things in the air are dressing, not creatures** (`cube_game::air`): pollen that drifts on the same wind and flares crossing a shaft of sun, leaves coming down in the woods, butterflies over the meadows, birds wheeling in flocks high up. They are kept in a box round the camera, the simulation does not know of them, and nothing reacts to them; a bird that can be hunted is a creature and belongs to `cube_sim`. About 330 small meshes in all.
- **Enough creatures to meet** (`Sim::populate`): groups are by the island's area (280 creatures on this one, where there were 145), and what does no harm may live 22 m from the start, hunters 45 m, the hostile 70 m. At 60 m for all but the timid, a player at the start saw a rabbit in the grass and nothing else.
- **Far creatures are neither drawn nor animated** (`motion::far_off`, beyond 120 m), and only near ones have their feet set to the drawn ground. The simulation moves them all the same. With all 280 posed and skinned every frame the frame went from 17 ms to 23; found by switching one thing off at a time (creatures, foliage culling, foliage gaps), not by guessing: the suspects had been the foliage and the torch.
- **Small life has bodies and wings, not boxes** (2026-11-11): each is a rounded body (`air::ellipsoid`) with a head, a beak and a tail where it has them, and wings, fins and tails that are pointed swept blades (`air::blade_mesh`), hinged and beaten in code. `AIR_SHOW=1` sets one of each out still by the start to be looked at. They are built here and not by the creature pipeline, which cannot yet rig a wing or six legs.
- **Fish** (`air::fish`): four schools of seven keep to water near the camera that is deep enough, circling a hand's depth under the surface with tails beating, 13 to 35 cm long; now and then one leaps and a ring spreads where it did. Dressing like the birds: nothing eats them and they cannot be caught. Fish to catch are creatures and belong to `cube_sim`.
- **Small life is true to size and kept close** (2026-11-11). For an hour butterflies and birds were drawn two to three times life size so that they showed in pictures; the developer ruled that everything in the game is its real size, small life included. What stays from that hour is where they are, not how big: butterflies (9 cm across) within 15 m of the camera, small birds (12 cm) within 26 m and flying more often, five swallows (33 cm across) looping round the camera a few metres up by day, hawks (1.2 m across) wheeling at 35 to 50 m. They are small in a still picture, as they are in life; they are seen because they move.
- **Life in the air is sparing** (2026-11-11): the developer wants life, not a snowstorm. Pollen is seventy faint specks; fireflies come out from dusk, sixty of them in clusters near the camera, each lit for a moment every few seconds, green and small; leaves come down from the real trees near the camera, one now and then and a flurry from one crown when a gust passes. A light that follows the camera's exposure must start from 'not yet set', not 0: full night is an exposure of 0, and the fireflies never lit.
- **Small birds and dragonflies** (`air::songbirds`, `dragonflies`, 2026-11-11). A small bird lives on the ground near the camera: a few hops, a peck, a look round, now and then a low arcing flight elsewhere, wings folded when down; any walking creature within 4.5 m puts it up and it goes further. A dragonfly hangs over water, darts two metres in a sixth of a second and hangs again. Movement is what makes them read as alive: each has rests and bursts, never a steady glide.
- **Water reflects the land** (2026-11-11; `--no-reflect` for the old forward path). Screen-space reflections in Bevy 0.20 want the scene rendered deferred: the default opaque method is deferred; an observer puts the depth and deferred prepasses, `ScreenSpaceReflections` and temporal anti-aliasing on every camera the moment it is made (a camera one frame without them, with deferred materials about, panics in `bevy_pbr`); the wind extension gives its prepass shader as its deferred vertex shader; the water is opaque and near mirror-smooth. Two things made the first working try show only sky: the default settings skip any surface smoother than 0.08 (the water is 0.04), and ten steps do not reach a far bank (48 do). Grain in the reflection is what the temporal anti-aliasing is for. Frame times are unchanged.
- **Water is not seen through** (2026-11-11). Blended, it wrote no depth, so the mist was measured through it to the lake's bed and every contour of the bed showed as a stripe across the water (found by rendering once with `--reflect`, where the water is opaque and had none). Shallows are paler by colour instead. And nothing at or under the water's level is put in the big blocks of rough country: rounded up to a metre, the bed stood out of a lake in bars.
- **Water has shallows, a shoreline and a murk** (2026-11-11): the mesher makes it paler and clearer where it is under a metre deep and lighter still along the very edge; with the camera under the surface the haze becomes green-blue murk a few metres deep. Not done: reflections of the land in it, waves on the sea, anything flowing.
- **Animals keep their hours** (`Mind::about`, set each tick from the niche): out of them one mostly stays resting where it is, though it still flees and fights. Those of the dusk are about a good deal by day too.
- **Water moves** (`world::ripples`, `ripple`): a tile of crossing wave trains as a normal map, four metres to a repeat, slid downwind; smooth enough to throw the sun back. At a low sun it throws back too much and whites out: not fixed.
- **Not done**: wind that changes (it is one direction and strength, written in the shaders and in `air.rs`), water that moves. (Sound is done: [Sound](#sound).)

### Paint

- **A photographed grain takes painted shapes over it** (`texture::atlas_with`, 2026-11-11): grass, earth, bark, moss, litter and cut wood. A photograph of grass from a hand's breadth is true and, from where a person stands, an even speckle with nothing the eye can hold (the developer: "plain ground, plain trees"); the grain made in code has the shapes (strokes of blades in clumps, clods, furrows) and none of the fineness. The photograph is multiplied by the painted grain, so the ground has both. Stone, sand, pebbles and snow stay as photographed: painted over, they were worse. `--plain` still shows the paint alone.

- **One atlas of grains, made in code at start** (`cube_voxel::texture`, 2026-11-11): fifteen tiles of 128 texels (1.5 cm) with a border of 16 that repeats the tile's far side, and four smaller copies each half the last. The border is what lets the picture be drawn smooth and shrunk with distance without a tile taking colour from its neighbour. No art files, no engine types: the game wraps the bytes in two images.
- **A grain may be a photograph** (`texture::atlas_with`, `Photo`): the game looks for `assets/textures/NAME.jpg` and `NAME_n.jpg` for each grain and hands their texels in; `cube_voxel` still reads no files. A photograph is brought to the same mean as any tile, so the block's colour tints it and far levels need nothing new. Tiles are 512 texels (4 mm) with a border of 64, six levels.
- **A grain is three things**: brightness in broad shapes and fine ones (`texel`: blades, grit, the fibre of bark), hue (`hue`: dry and lush blades, russet and gold in fallen leaves, lichen and rust on rock, moss on bark), and relief (a normal map taken from the brightness, so a low sun rakes across bark and stone). The first grain was brightness alone in broad shapes, and the developer saw no texture at all: one colour made lighter and darker reads as plastic.
- **The mesher gives every corner a place in it** from where the corner is in the world, not in its cube: tops lie flat, sides stand upright, and the tile repeats every eight cells, so bits, whole cells and the far levels of detail all show the same picture at the same place.
- **A grain multiplies the block's colour and every tile averages 0.8 in each of red, green and blue**; the mesher divides the block's colour by that. So the far levels need no other colours, and grain can be changed without touching a block.
- **Still one material and one draw a chunk.** Not done: grain that differs by biome or wetness, more than one tile to a grain (a wall repeats every two metres), roughness maps, and merging faces (the per-cube shade is small now but still in the vertices).
- **Every scene shot says what it cost** (`pace`): the middle and worst frame of the last sixty, the terrain faces in view, and what drew them. A scene has no window, and with none the loop idles at sixty a second: until 2026-11-11 every pace read 17 ms whatever the frame cost. The idle wait is now nothing in a scene, so the number is the work. Frame times compare only on the same machine; faces in view compare anywhere.

## The world

Built 2026-11-10. `cube_sim::terrain` makes it; `cargo world survey` and `map` judge it.

- **An island from a seed**, 384 m across (`SEED`, `RADIUS`). The same seed is always the same world, in the game, in tests and in `cargo world`. An island because a finite world needs an edge, and sea to the horizon is an edge nobody questions. It is a stand-in: the world is to be procedural and far larger, a small version of a real one (see Where the world goes).
- **Every size comes from one table, in metres** (`cube_sim::scale`), measured against a person of 1.75 m: the landforms (how far across, how tall, how rough), how tall each kind of tree stands, how much room it has, the classes of rock, the heights where rock and snow begin. `terrain` and `plants` carry no sizes of their own, and `cargo world survey` holds what was made to bars in the same file (the middle tree at least 8 people tall, the largest rock ten times the smallest, 65% of lowland meadow and forest easy ground). The reason: the world was scaled up three times by hand, a number at a time, and each time something was left behind and the person looked like a giant on a model. Scale is a ratio between things, so it is kept in one place and measured.
- **The height is those landforms added up** (`terrain::column`, a pure function of a point and the seed): a lowland; smooth hills over it (two layers of noise, since every layer added makes the ground as steep again, and lumps every few metres are what made it a model); one mountain and one lesser height, placed by the seed well out from the start and each a bell, so the outer third is foothills and the middle is climbing; ridges and ledges on the mountain only, the ledges wandering and missing from whole faces so they are not rings; a river in a broad valley; the shore. The first mountains were wherever a noise ran high: on this island that was most of the rim, as flat-topped walls standing straight up from the plain (seen with `cargo world grade`, which was written to find out why only a third of the lowland was easy ground).
- **Biomes come from height, slope and a moisture noise**: shore, meadow, forest, rock (high or steep), snow. A biome decides the surface block, what is planted (broadleaf trees, pines higher up, bushes, boulders) and who lives there.
- **`Land` is kept beside the voxels**: every column's height and biome, and the trees. Placing creatures and measuring the world read it, not the cells.
- **Things are their real size, drawn in cubes.** An oak stands 13 to 24 m, a birch 11 to 19, a pine 17 to 34, a willow 9 to 14, six and a half metres apart in a forest; every other measure of a tree (trunk, limbs, crown) is a part of its height. The first trees were 5 to 8 m and read as saplings; the second were 9 to 18 m and still made the person a giant.
- **A tree is a tree, not a pole with a ball on it** (`cube_sim::plants`). A trunk flares into roots at the ground and tapers upward with a lean; limbs leave it, bend and fork; foliage is separate clumps at the ends of the limbs, so sky shows through and no two trees are alike. Five kinds: oak (broad, heavy-limbed), birch (slender, pale bark, light and high), pine (whorls of drooping boughs under a point, dead stubs below the green), willow (short and thick by water, hanging in curtains), and the dead snag. Which grows where follows the land: conifers high and on rock, willows within a few metres of water, the rest mixed. The first trees were a square column and one blob, and from the ground a forest was a field of sticks.
- **What is made by the generator is shaped to the bit where a cell is too coarse.** A rock is stone with seven to ten flat faces struck off it, cut at 5 cm. Rocks come in three classes (stones underfoot 0.3 to 0.8 m, boulders 1 to 2.6 m, the rare great one 3.2 to 6 m that is a landmark) and four forms (a block, a slab lying flat, a tooth standing up, a hump mostly buried), each sat on the ground under it with smaller pieces against it and stones fallen round a large one. Built from whole cells a boulder was a pile of crates; as rounded lumps of one size they were uniform orbs. Terrain, trunks and foliage stay whole cells: a face of a broken cell is 25 squares where a whole one is one.
- **A wood has an understory.** Between the trees, on a finer grid of their own, stand young ones three to seven metres tall with their crowns at eye level, bushes from knee to head high, fallen logs and stumps. Without them everything green was overhead.
- **Ground cover is drawn as blades, not cubes, and is walked through.** Long grass, flowers, ferns and reeds are cells that are not solid. A blade of grass is two planes crossed on the diagonals of a bit (5 cm), seen from both sides, painted with the grass's grain and dark at its foot; a tuft is five to eight of them, and they grow in patches over much of a meadow. As five-sided boxes, three to a tuft, a meadow was sparse sticks; thickened as boxes it was more faces than all the land under it (2.35 million drawn from the start against a bar of 2).
- **Trees and bushes are solid blocks.** A crown starts 4.5 m up so the golem walks under it. Creatures will not climb onto leaves; the player can.
- **Judged by numbers first** (`cargo world survey`): water 20% to 60%, each of meadow, forest and rock at least 6%, 400 trees, ground at least 40 m high, cliffs on 3% to 35% of the land, half the land reached from the start on foot and nine tenths by swimming, at most 2 million faces drawn from the start, and the scale bars above. `cargo world grade` says where the ground is easy, steep and cliff by height, and draws it. A seed that fails one is not a world to ship; seed 6 passes and is the default.
- **Not yet:** caves and overhangs (the land is a height map; a cave is a set, [Light](#light)). (Shaped blocks and textures are done: [The ground's blocks](#the-grounds-blocks), [Paint](#paint).)
- **Where the world goes** (decided 2026-11-10): procedural and much larger, a small version of a real world, not one island held whole in memory. What that needs, in order: `Land` answering for any column from the seed instead of holding an array of all of them; cells made a chunk at a time near the player and dropped behind, with what was changed kept; trees and rocks placed by a rule that gives the same answer whichever chunk asks first (a tree's crown crosses chunks); where mountains, plains, forests and seas are coming from slow maps (height of the land, warmth, wet) instead of one placed mountain; creatures placed and put away as the player moves; and beyond the last chunks one coarse mesh from the height map. The scale table is what such a world is generated from, and `column` is already a pure function of a point.

- **What the ground is at a place comes from what is there** (`terrain::fill`, 2026-11-11), not from its biome alone:
  - **Trails** (`terrain::worn`, `Land::trail`): the level lines of a slow bent noise, so they wander and fork and one passes within a hundred metres of anywhere; a metre of bare earth, trodden grass each side, long grass along the edge. Nothing stands in one (`Land::thing`), which is what makes it a way through a wood. Only on meadow and forest floor that is not steep. They are not yet roads between places. The larger animals go by them (`mind::think`): of the first places one thinks of roaming to, it takes only one that lies on a trail, so `cargo world run` finds about one in six standing on a trail at any moment where trails are a fiftieth of the ground.
  - **Under a tree is that tree's ground**: `fill` gathers the trees whose crowns are over a region, and a column knows how far under a crown it is. Bare earth at the foot of a trunk; fallen leaf in drifts thickening toward it (a bed of needles under a pine), under a lone meadow oak as much as in a wood; in its shade the grass thins and ferns and mushrooms come up.
  - **Forest floor**: turf, leaf drifts, moss where it is dampest, ferns in colonies, mushrooms in troops on the litter. **Meadow**: flowers in drifts some metres over, long grass in its own patches, scrapes of bare earth, and the grass itself dry and straw-coloured in stretches and lush in others (`mesh::sward`). **Shore**: beds of pebbles in the sand, reeds in stands at the water, marram on the dry sand behind. **Rock**: grass in the cracks of low rock.
  - `make floors` pictures a trail, a forest floor, a meadow and a shore in one sheet; `cargo world find trail` says where an open stretch of trail is.

## What is placed is data

The developer's rule (2026-11-11): what varies is data, and a new kind of anything that is placed in the world by rule is added without touching the code that places it. Where this stands:

- **Creatures**: `assets/creatures/ID/creature.toml` (`[life]`: temper, habitat, water, cover, hours, numbers, voice; `[built]` for those forge makes).
- **Trees**: `assets/world/flora.toml` (`cube_sim::flora`). A kind is its name, its habit (one of five shapes, the only part in code: `plants::tree`), its height, girth, spread and reach, its bark and leaf, and how common it is on low ground, high ground and by water. Beech, aspen and spruce were added as data alone. The land picks by site from the table (`flora::pick`), the far stand-ins and the ground under a crown ask the kind what it is.
- **The ground and what grows on it**: `assets/world/land.toml` (`cube_sim::cover`). How thick trees, undergrowth and rocks stand in each biome; what the undergrowth is, by shares; and two lists of rules, for the top of the soil and for the cell over it. A rule is tests of a place (how worn by a trail, how far under a crown and whether a conifer's, how damp, what it stands on, wet or dry, slope, height, a patch of slow noise, a chance) and what to put where they all hold; the first that fits is taken. `terrain::fill` asks and places. Woodland flowers in the glades were added as one rule and no code.
- **Which biome a place is**: `[[biome]]` in `land.toml` (2026-11-11): an ordered list of rules on how far it stands over the sea, how steep it is and how damp, the first that fits. Moving a snow line or making steep ground forest is a number there. The survey of the land came out the same to the byte before and after.
- **Not yet data**: the list of biomes itself (six, the `Biome` enum, named in some two hundred places: a seventh is still code); the sizes and lines in `scale`; rocks' classes there; the small life of the air (niches in `ecology`, numbers in `air`).

## What lives in it

- **Numbers are made by the island, not set** (`Sim::day`, 2026-11-11; the developer: "if the simulation isn't like real life we have failed. That means doing more than just putting a few creatures here"). The creatures near the player live second by second; the rest, a thousand and more, live by the day: the old die, what eats meat must find prey within its range or starve, what eats plants dies for want of food where it outnumbers what its ground feeds, a few die of the winter, pairs have young on the first day of spring (fewer the fuller the ground), and a group grown to twice its size sends half to find ground of their own. Nothing dead is put back: until this day the dead returned where they had lived after two and a half minutes.
  - What a kind eats, its litter, its years and what its ground feeds are data (`[life]`: `diet`, `litter`, `lifespan`, `capacity`, `share`), and unsaid they follow from its size: the small breed fast and die young. A hunter ranges two and a half times as far as what it hunts and keeps a territory that holds one group; a pack shares a large kill. A thing of stone or one not alive (`diet = "none"`) neither eats, breeds nor ages.
  - **`cargo world years N`** runs the island by the day for N years and prints each kind's numbers year by year, with what was born and what died of what; it fails if a kind that began as six or more dies out or one grows past five times its start. Its first run killed everything in a year: the island had been stocked with a hunter to every two of the hunted (84 wolves, 83 goblins). Hunters are now a tenth to a half as common (`share`), and eight years hold: rabbits and squirrels rise to what their ground feeds, deer and ponies fall a third under the wolves and steady, wolves number four to nine.
  - The young are born small and grow (`Sim::grown`, drawn by `motion`).
  - **What is awake lives by the same needs** (`mind`, `Sim::step`): it grows hungry and thirsty by the second at the day's rates. Thirsty, it walks to the nearest water within sixty metres and drinks. What eats plants grazes because it is hungry, little when full and nearly always when empty; two hours' grazing is a meal. What hunts hunts from hunger: fed, it lets a rabbit go by, and a person too, unless they come within a few steps (what is hostile needs no reason); a kill feeds it for days. `cargo world run` says how often each kind drank.
  - **Judged over 24 years, not 8, and on several seeds** (2026-11-11). Eight years passed while goblins and wolves were still climbing sixfold, ogres and trolls were dying out unbred, and on some seeds the deer and ponies were being eaten to nothing: none of it showed in eight. What holds it now: a hunter hunts the ground it keeps and two and a half times beyond, not the island; the fewer the hunted there, the harder the hunt, by the square (the last few are hard to find); a kill feeds by its bulk, so a wolf on a deer does not hunt again for weeks; what hunts rears young as it has fed. And in the creatures' own files (`[life]`): `meal` (a troll eats every 20 days, not 6), `range` (goblin camps keep 260 m apart) and `capacity`. `cargo world years 24` fails a kind that dies out or has not settled (within a third of eight years before), and under any kind that fell by half says who hunted it.
  - **What is awake starves as what is out of sight does** (2026-11-11): a meat-eater near the player hungers by the second; past three meals missed it loses strength, and at four (`life::STARVES`, the number `day` uses) it lies down and dies where it is. Until then an awake hunter was hungered twice (by the second and by the day) and could not die of it.
  - **What grows is eaten and grows back** (`Sim::forage`, 2026-11-11). On each piece of ground 64 m across, what grazes there takes of it each day by its size (height squared: a moose forty rabbits' worth) and what is left grows by the season, fastest in spring, not at all in winter. On ground grazed lean (under 30% left) what grazes starves and rears fewer young, whatever its kind: so the deer and the ponies, which a count of their own kind let rise to 400 and 500, are held near 100 and 130 by what they and the rest have eaten. Over 24 years four fifths of the grazing is left on the whole, and the barest piece is eaten to nothing in its worst winter. `cargo world years` says both.
  - **What grazes in sight is seen to eat**: it takes from the grazing as what is out of sight does, and about once a second the tuft, flower or fern nearest its head is gone from the ground (`Sim::eaten`: the simulation says which cells and the game takes them, since the simulation does not change the world). `cargo world run` counts them: some 400 in five minutes round the start. What is eaten is not kept: it stands again when that ground is next made.
  - **Ground eaten down is seen to be, and feeds nothing in sight** (2026-11-14). The grazing left on a piece of ground was a number that starved what was out of sight and nothing else: the grass stood as thick on ground a hundred deer had bared as on ground none had touched, and a grazer in sight fed on it as on a meadow. A region is now made with less growing on it by how much grazing was left there when the day began (`terrain::graze`, from `Sim::pasture`): none gone at four fifths left and over (`LUSH`), all of meadow's and wood's tufts, flowers and ferns gone at nothing, each by its own place (and the same all day, so a region made again is the same). It grows back as the number does, when the ground is next made. And on ground with under a twentieth left (`BARE`) what grazes in sight is no less hungry for it, and moves on. `GRAZED=0.3` on any `cargo sandbox` makes all the ground so, to be looked at.
  - **What keeps a den goes to ground in it** (`den = true` in `[life]`, `Agent::under`, 2026-11-14; rabbit, badger, fox, adder). Its den is where it lives (`Mind::home`, which for these is not moved to wherever it last got away to). Frightened within thirty metres of it (`mind::DEN`) and not with what it runs from in the way, it bolts for it and at its mouth goes under for half a minute or so; out of its hours it goes home and lies up in it, twenty seconds at a time for as long as it stays out of them. Under the ground it is not seen by anything, not struck and not drawn, and does nothing. The game draws a burrow where one is near the eye (`motion::dens`: a heap of earth with a dark mouth; `DEN_TRACE=1` says where each is). Before, what fled ran until it was let be, and a rabbit a wolf could outrun had nowhere to go.
  - **A bird that roosts goes up onto a bough out of its hours** (`roost = true` in `[life]`, `Mind::perch`, 2026-11-14; crow, hawk, owl, pheasant). The first time it wants one it looks for the lowest bough near where it lives: in each column within 24 m (`ROOST`), the first wood from two and a half to eight metres over the ground (whole cells, or a thin bough's bits) with a metre and a half of room over it, leaf being room; the nearest such. It flies up short of it, comes in over the wood itself and down onto it, backs out if what is overhead stops it, and sits until its hours come round, when it leaves the bough and glides home. One it cannot get onto in half a minute it gives up, and lies up on the ground. What was learned getting there (`ROOST_TRACE=1` says where a bird was when it gave up): sent to the highest wood in a column, a crow was caught under the crown of a tree of twenty metres; over a thin bough's cell but not its wood, a pheasant hung with nothing under it; and a fox stood under a roosting pheasant's tree with no way on, so what has gone up out of reach is not chased. `cargo world run --seconds 660` ends at night and counts them: 6 crows of 6 and 4 pheasants of 4 to 6 on two islands, half of each on a third.
  - **A creature finds its way out of a notch in the drawn ground** (`mind::steer`, 2026-11-14). A body stands on the ground as it is drawn, and on a steep hillside that is blocks of up to a metre, so in a few columns in a thousand it stands half a metre to a metre down inside its cells (`WORLD_VERBOSE=1 cargo world drawn` lists them with the heights round each). A mind looked for its way from its feet, where every way was then a wall three cells high: on seed 5 a fox ran into such a notch and stood in it for the rest of the day (`boxed-in` a quarter of the run). The way on is now looked for from the top of the cells it stands in, and out of a notch deeper than it steps it jumps.
  - **Not yet**: nests; a den is drawn on the ground and not dug into it.

- **A kind keeps its own ground** (`Sim::populate`): no group settles within 80 m of another of its kind (30 m for those that live alone). Herds went wherever suited them and three of ponies stood within a stone's throw: one cluster, to the developer. The pony is 1.6 m tall, not 1.45.

Built 2026-11-10. `cube_sim::life` and `mind`; `cargo world run` judges it.

- **The simulation owns every creature.** `Sim` holds an agent for each: its body, who drives it (the keys, its own mind, nobody) and what it is doing (`Act`, which is a clip's name). The game keeps a model in step with each agent and never moves one itself. So the same tick runs in the window, in a test and in `cargo world run`, and a server later.
- **A mind is a temper and a handful of states**, not a behaviour tree. Tempers, set in `creature.toml` `[life]`: *timid* (rabbit, deer) grazes and runs from people, hunters and monsters; *hunter* (wolf) runs timid creatures down, then is spent for a while; *wary* (bear, boar) faces whatever comes within 7 m and hits it if it comes closer; *hostile* (goblin, troll) goes for people on sight. A deer bolts from a wolf at 12 m and lets a bear come to 5. Enough for a world that looks alive and for the player to have something to run from; more is added when a need (hunger, sleep) asks for it.
- **No path planning.** A creature heads for a point and looks a step ahead: it turns aside from walls, drops and water it would have to swim, trying ways further round until one is open, and its own box must be able to move that way. At its ease it takes half-metre steps and drops of a metre, so it stays on the ground it knows; fleeing or chasing it jumps a metre and drops three. Good enough on open ground; a creature can still be walled in, and then it waits.
- **Roaming keeps to a home**: a group shares one, placed in the kind's biome. A creature that flees settles where it stops.
- **Nothing dies yet.** A blow is a shove and a flinch (`hurt`). Health, death and what the player hits back with come with combat.
- **A swimmer climbs out over a bank** up to half a metre above its waist. Without it a steep-sided river was a trap, for the player too; the headless run found it.
- **Random choices come from one seeded generator** in the `Sim`, and agents are visited in id order, so a seed replays exactly (tested).
- **Judged by numbers** (`cargo world run`): no creature ends a tick inside the ground or falls out of the world, each kind walks into something for at most 8% of the time, is walled in for at most 10%, swims for at most 5%, and goes at least 3 m.

**A bird flies from what frightens it** (`flies = true` in `[life]`, 2026-11-13; `Sim::step`, `Intent.lift`): fleeing, it climbs to 5 m over the ground and goes at 6.5 m/s at the least, faster than anything hunts on foot; nothing strikes what is more than its own height and 0.8 m over it. With nothing to flee it glides down at 1.8 m/s toward where it lives, and never onto water (the first crows flew out to sea, settled and swam). On the wing the mind steers straight: steering is for feet and reads the air under a bird as a drop. Its home is not moved by a flight, as a walker's is. No game code: aloft is the `fall` clip, which for a bird is flight. Not done: a bird whose home has the player standing on it goes out and back for as long as they stay; birds do not perch.

## Fighting, and what a person carries

Built 2026-11-11, in the simulation (`cube_sim::life`, `items`) and tested there; the game shows it (`cube_game::hud`).

- **What is in hand is seen in the hand** (`cube_game::held`, `assets/world/held.toml`, 2026-11-11): a few boxes seated in the closed hand (forge reads each model's hands, `[grip]` in `meta.toml`: where the handle lies across the palm, and each finger bone's turn closed round it; pinned to the hand's bone at a hand-tuned angle it went wherever the wrist's roll went when the motion changed, and the hand was open), so what moves the arm moves the thing; the torch's light comes from its head. Each thing's grip (`turn`, `shift`) is data. A thing may say how the arm carries it (`arm`: the way the upper arm and forearm point), applied after the clip has posed the body and before the pose is used, each bone by the least turn: the torch is held up this way, since every clip hangs the arm and a torch at the end of it lit his knees. Only figures on Tripo's humanoid skeleton have a hand to find (by the bone's name). In the first person, whose body is not drawn, the thing is hung on the camera, low and to the right, riding with his steps and thrown across the view with a blow. A kind may be seen to carry a thing (`holds = "Club"` in its `[life]`: goblin, troll, ogre), made as big as it is (`fits`). A thing's size is kept to the hand each frame, not set once: on the frame a model arrives its bones are not placed. A thing may be the left hand's (`left`: the bow, held out before him, strung). The arm's ways are in the body's frame with +z ahead: set with -z ahead at first, the torch was held behind the shoulder and nobody had looked from the side with him walking. A blow is the clip's (a sword stroke a person animated: [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#where-motion-comes-from)) with whatever is in the hand; posing the arm over a clip was tried for a day and the two fought. A blow struck on the move is seen over the gait: the legs and hips of a person's skeleton are a group its clips' graph can leave alone, and the blow is in the graph a second time leaving them alone, played far heavier than the gait under it (`creatures::legs`; before, the blow landed and nothing of it was seen, the gait being the only clip played). Only the bow has a pose of its own (`blow` for its arm, `draw` for the other, `looses` for the arrow that is seen to fly). Who shoots stands (the idle clip): the blow's lunge took the bow down with it. Nothing is in hand while its bearer swims (the sword was waved overhead at every stroke; the torch goes out with it). Before the eye too: the bow stands upright at the left and comes back as it is drawn, the torch is thrust straight out, anything else cuts across the view (`cargo sandbox --scene F.png --play --view first --script '0.1 stop hold5; 1.0 use' --strip 4 --after 0.9 --every 0.12` shows one). Not done: a thing in each hand at once.
- **Health is by size**: a rabbit 22, a wolf 56, a bear 91, a golem 345; a person 100. A blow takes what the striker's size gives (4 + 9 a metre of height), or for a person what is in hand: fists 6, a torch 9, a shovel 12, a short sword 28.
- **A blow lands a third of a second into the swing** on the nearest thing within a metre and a half in front. Minds time their own; a driven creature asks with `Sim::swing`.
- **The dead lie, are gone, and return.** At no health a creature falls (`Act::Die`), lies 25 s, and is removed; 150 s later another of its kind comes to live where it did, so hunting and being hunted do not empty the world. A person wakes at home after five seconds, whole. Left in peace eight seconds, anything mends two a second.
- **A pack is 24 slots, the first eight a bar to hand, one of those in the hand** (`items::Pack`). A person sets out with a short sword, a torch, a shovel and six berries. `Sim::use_held` is the one way a thing is used: a weapon swings, berries are eaten for 15 health (only by the hurt), the shovel asks the game to dig. The torch is lit while it is the thing in hand.
- **Keys**: left button uses what is in hand; 1 to 8 take a slot, Q and E step along the bar, T takes the torch; I opens the pack (click a slot, then another, to change them over); F1 opens the admin panel (time, season and speed; heal, hurt, give, go to water or wood or meadow or rock; put any creature down ahead; nothing hurts me). In a script: `use`, `torch`, `hold3`.
- **`make fight`** runs it all in the real game with no window: a script puts a goblin down in front of the player (`spawn=goblin`) and swings; the trace must show the player hurt, the goblin killed, and the swing and the goblin heard.
- **Tested without a window**: the rules in `cube_sim` tests; from the game, a scripted `--play --scene` prints `health=`, `held=` and `kills=` in its trace. The bar, the pack and the admin panel are drawn into a scene with `--hud bar|pack|admin`, and two of them are in `make slice`.
- **A body stands on the ground as it is drawn** (2026-11-12, `World::stood`, after the developer asked why the voxels were not easy colliders). The cells are 25 cm cubes; the ground is drawn as blocks of other sizes at heights of their own (`mesh::Eased`), and until now a body was stopped by the cells: 10 cm from the drawn ground at the 95th column of a hundred, 75 cm at the worst, a drawn step a metre where the cells' was 25 to 50 cm. The model was then moved to the drawn ground under its middle and each foot to the drawn ground under it, and every such patch left a leg in the ground or a foot in the air somewhere. Now the simulation's one test of what is solid (`Body::blocked`) takes natural ground to be solid up to where its column is drawn, which is always a whole number of 5 cm bits, so stepping, falling and walls work as they did, on the drawn blocks. A column's drawn height is worked out as the mesh works it out, kept by chunk, and forgotten within a chunk of any cell written. `cargo world drawn` (in `make prove`): a body let fall on 3,705 places rests on the highest ground drawn under it at all but 5. What follows from it: a drawn block a metre high is a wall where its cells were a stair (the player ran 81 m in `make pace` where he ran 101); a body is put on the ground if a tick begins with it inside (creatures are still put down by the cells). Only a world made as land is made does this (`World::eased`): one built by hand in a test is its cells. Not done: what is not natural ground and is drawn moved with it (the bits of a dug hole, a rock) still stops a body where its cells are; a ray (the camera, a spadeful) still meets the cells. Feet: the body is let down until the foot over the highest ground stands on it, and no further (`feet`); `make legs` says how near the ground any knee or ankle comes.
- **A small creature's box is no wider than it is, and a ledge is not a slope** (2026-11-14, from a rabbit seen standing nose down on the air beside a terrace). What stands on the ground stands on whatever is under its box, and no box was under 20 cm across: a rabbit 10 cm wide stood with its middle and all four feet past a rim (`FEET_TRACE=1`: the ground under every foot half a metre down). A box now goes down to 10 cm across (`life::SLIGHT`). And where the ground under a four-legged body's fore feet is below that under its hind feet by four fifths of what they are apart and more, it is at an edge and is not pitched to it as to a hillside (`feet::LEDGE`).
- **Gaits are mixed by speed, in step** (`creatures::drive`, 2026-11-12). Going over the ground a creature's idle, walk, run and sprint are all in play, each with a share that goes by how fast it is going, each at the same part of its own stride; the share of the faster gait is the one at which the mix covers the ground at the speed being gone (each gait's own true speed is in meta.toml), so no clip is played fast or slow to fit and the stride is what changes. Before, one clip was chosen by the gait asked for and played from a third to two and a half times its rate to fit, and one gait became the next in a fifth of a second whatever the legs were doing. forge starts every gait where one fore foot passes the other, so they agree (`FORGE_TRACE=1` prints which foot leads through each). The shares are kept by `drive` itself: Bevy's `AnimationTransitions` knows one clip coming in and the ones going out, and sets their weights every frame. Any other clip (a blow, a leap, a fall) comes in alone over the creature's blend time, as before. Not done: clips for starting, stopping and turning on the spot (the library in use has none); a leap is still three clips in a row.
- **How alive it is has a number** (`cargo world run`, the `alive` line, 2026-11-12, after the developer: "the simulation, both programmatically and visually, needs to be alive"). Of the creatures lived near the watcher: how much of their time is standing about, lying up out of their hours, feeding, running; and a minute, how many set out for somewhere with a reason, drank, took fright, hunted. Watched for five minutes before, every kind stood 53% to 83% of the time, eight of sixty-nine drank and nothing was hunted. What changed in the mind: thirst sends a creature to water three times a day and not once; one that sets off to drink is followed by those of its kind near it that could do with one, and one that bolts is bolted with (only from what is still near, or two of a herd keep each other running); what grazes goes over the ground as it feeds, a few steps and eats again, and takes four hours over a meal; what hunts and is hungry walks toward the nearest prey anywhere about until it is near enough to run it down, where it hunted only what came to it; out of its hours a creature goes home to lie up; water it cannot get to it gives up on. Now, on three islands: standing about 20% to 30%, feeding 30%, five to seven a minute drink where there is water, a hunt a minute. Bars: at most 35% standing about, from two to sixty errands a minute. At rest a few seconds out of its hours, or settled for a long rest when fed and watered (one rest in four, half a minute to a minute), a creature lies down (`Act::Lie`, the made `lie` clip: belly to the ground, legs folded under, head low): a sixth to a fifth of the time watched. Only a creature marked `lies = true` goes down; the rest lower their heads and stand, since on some rigs the same pose is a wolf sitting or a deer's body hung between straight legs, and no measure told those from a goat lying (goat, bear, boar-beast, badger and squirrel were looked at and marked). How a creature rests is its own (`rests` in creature.toml, held to by `make features`): `side` (where the wolf's fall ends, held and breathing: the wolf), `belly` (goat, bear, boar-beast, badger, squirrel), `crouch` (the beaver), `curl`, `stands`. The developer: not every creature sleeps on its side; some crouch on all fours, foxes and cats curl up. A leg folds at where the leg proper begins and the joint half way down it (`clips::folds_at`), not at the first two bones of its chain: Tripo hangs some legs from a stub of the spine, and bending the stub left the fox, deer and rabbit standing when lowered. That was taken for rigs that could not fold, and rigs were about to be bought again for it; the skeleton printed by `cargo forge inspect` showed five and six bones a leg. With it the rabbit and fox crouch and the deer half does. Still standing: the lynx (its fore legs stay straight) and the hedgehog (legs of two bones). The curl bends too little to be seen and tore the fox: nothing curls yet. No free resting clip exists to carry instead ([research](research/2026-11-12-open-animation.md)). Not done: a hunt seldom ends in a kill in sight; nothing calls, plays, follows its mother or sleeps to be seen.
- **Bodies moved by their skeleton and feet, no clips** (`cube_anim`, 2026-11-12). The order for anything new: the arithmetic with tests, then boxes (`make limbs`, `make person`), then the model. The player is moved this way as the game runs (`cube_game::stride`); the wolf has the four-legged walker's skeleton and its gaits written into clips ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md#a-four-legged-animal-on-our-own-skeleton-plan--walked-the-wolf)). Tried and removed: fitting the walker to Tripo's own skeleton as the game ran (`--by-feet`): its joints are not where an animal's are, and legs went wrong.
- **Aiming** (the right button held, 2026-11-12): the camera comes in over the right shoulder and looks past him, a mark shows the middle of the picture, and he turns to face that way, so a blow or a shot goes where the mark is. A spadeful goes where the mark is too: found along the camera's own line and held to his reach from his eyes (from his eyes along the camera's way it fell beside the mark). The right button still guards; building with the shovel moved to B and the middle button.
- **A chunk that has changed is drawn as it was until what replaces it is ready** (`Drawn::old`), and its new mesh is begun at once, not when the pass over the chunks next finds it: taken away first, every spadeful was a frame or more of no ground (the developer: "using the shovel also causes a flicker").
- **A kill gives meat** to a killer who carries a pack, by the size of what was killed; eaten, it mends 35. **A guard** (the right button held, with anything but the shovel in hand) turns seven tenths of a blow that comes from in front.
- **A bow** is in the pack from the start: its blow reaches thirty metres in a line five degrees either side, where a blade's reaches a step and sweeps wide (`Item::reach`); it takes 16. No arrow is drawn or flies: what is in line when the shot lands is hit.
- **An arrow is seen** to fly from the archer when the bow is loosed (`player::arrows`); what it hit was already decided by the simulation.
- **The player's state is saved** (`Sim::save`, `load`, tested): when it is, where they are, their health and pack, as a few lines of text in `terra-cube.save` where the game is run from. F5 writes it, F9 reads it, `--continue` starts from it; in a script, `save` and `load`. The land is the seed's and is not written, so what was dug or built is lost, as are the creatures' whereabouts.
- **Armour**: a leather jerkin, while it is anywhere in the pack, takes a quarter off every blow (the admin panel gives one; none is found in the world yet).
- **A blow lands on a part of a body** (2026-11-13; `Kind::struck`, `life::Part`). Forge measures a box for the head, the trunk, each limb and the tail from the skin as the model stands (`check::parts`: a corner of skin is of the part whose bone it follows most, or the nearest such bone up from it) and writes them to `meta.toml` (`[[hit]]`: metres from the ground under its middle, x to its left, z the way it faces); `--bodies` draws them. A person who is aiming (the right button) strikes along the line they look, and what that line goes through first is what is struck: half as much again to the head, seven tenths to a limb, half to a tail. Not aiming, a blow is at the trunk, as every blow was: tried along a line from the shoulder, a blow from in front found the head for being in the way, and every fight was half as deadly again without anyone meaning it. An animal's bite is at the trunk. Tried in the real game against a goblin (the trace's `landed=`): not aiming, every blow at its trunk; aiming, an arm, its head, an arm. They are measured three times, where it has the clips: standing, with its head down to feed, and lying (`when` in `[[hit]]`; a wolf's head is 0.48 to 0.85 m up standing and 0.13 to 0.55 m feeding), and the one that is used goes by what it is doing. Between those they do not follow its clips: a leg mid-stride is struck where it stands.
- **A kill's meat lies with it, to be taken by hand** (`Sim::butcher`, 2026-11-14). It was put in the pack of whoever killed it as it fell, from wherever the blow or the arrow came, and nothing a creature left was ever on the ground. It is on the body now (`Agent::meat`, one and more by its size); a person within arm's reach of it takes it with empty hands, by the key that takes up a hunk (F, when there is no hunk to take and nothing to heave); a body with its meat on it lies two minutes, not twenty-five seconds. What hunts to eat eats its kill where it lies and leaves none. `make fight` takes a goblin's.
- **Not done**: hides, bones or anything else of a creature's; saving what was dug.

## Sound

- **The air is quiet beds and single small sounds, not loops** (2026-11-11). The developer, after the first round: "some of the sounds are still ambiently too loud or too simple and repetitive". Measured before changing anything, a five-minute stand in the noon meadow gave three things: an eighteen-second loop of birdsong going round, the hawks overhead calling every few seconds (each bird had its own timer, and there are a dozen), and a herd of horses neighing one recording seventeen times. So, by design and not by one slider:
  - *Loudness.* `Mix::air` is 0.3 (was 0.45), and the beds' own weights are wind 0.3 (swelling and falling between 0.35 and 1 of that by `gust`, three slow sines that never fall into step), the night's insects 0.14, water 0.22. With the files levelled to -27 dB that puts a meadow's wind between -62 and -53 dB, a summer night's bed at -56, a river at its bank at -53, against the walker's own footfall at -42 (mean over the file; its loudest fifth of a second -40). The small sounds are set so the nearest bird (never under 18 m) peaks under a footfall. The reason for these numbers and no lower: the air is still to be heard when standing still, and the admin slider goes to 1.5 for an ear to settle it.
  - *Repetition.* No bed is one file: each is a set (`amb_wind_1`..`_6`, 176 s of three recordings; `amb_water_1`..`_4`, 114 s of two) of which one plays, and every 24 to 54 s another comes in under it over seven seconds at a speed a little its own (0.94 to 1.06) while the first goes out. A bed that is not heard here is not played. The looped birdsong is gone: birds are single calls, a phrase of one to four from one perch (a real songbird's when one is out and near, so the one seen is the one heard), of a kind that suits how wooded it is (`SINGERS`: meadowlark and sparrow in the open, wren and chickadees between, warbler, nuthatch and thrush in the wood), ten kinds with three to seven calls each. No set plays the same file twice running (`Bank::pick`), and a set of one file is thrown further off its pitch (12%, not 7).
  - *Small sounds*, each seldom, from somewhere about, and how often is `ecology::ambience`'s to say (new fields `leaves`, `insects`, `frogs`, `owls`, tested): a raven, crow, woodpecker's drum or dove from 45 to 95 m; the hawks, one call between them every minute or two; leaves when the wind gusts, where there are trees; grasshoppers, a cicada and a bee low in the grass on a summer's day in the open; frogs within reach of water from dusk; an owl in the wood at night; and the night's crickets one by one, up to five, each at its own place, pace and pitch, chirping in bouts.
  - *They are placed by our own rule, not the engine's.* The engine's sound falls with the square of the distance past 5 m, which has a bird at 50 m unheard; the small sounds are played from 4 m off in their direction (so they are heard from that side) at a loudness that halves with each doubling of distance past 12 m (`Bank::far`).
  - *Creatures' calls have a budget.* No call within six seconds of another's, a kind silent for a minute after one of it has called, and the kind that called last waits for another kind or three minutes: a herd is many animals with one voice.
  - *Judged by measurement* (`make sound`, in `make prove`): `tools/audio/listen.sh PLACE HOUR [DAY]` stands in a place for five minutes of the real game with its clock run six times as fast (`--speed 6`; five minutes of the game are five hours of its day), and `tools/audio/mix.py` reads the trace (every sound now says how loud it reached the ear and when, and each bed says so once a second) with every file's own level: how many different files, the longest run of one file, the air's mean and loudest fifth of a second against a footfall in the same run, and how much of the time a small sound is sounding. Bars: mean 6 dB under a footfall, peak no more than 3 over one, no file more than twice running, eight files. Meadow in summer from ten in the morning: 28 small files in 49 plays and 6 beds, longest run 1, air -53 dB against a footfall at -42, a small sound 18% of the time (most of it grasshoppers and a cicada; 7% before they were added). Shore from six in the evening: -50 dB, 50% of the time (the crickets). Runs differ by a decibel or two: what plays depends on the frame.
  - *Where they are from*: the sound libraries of Yellowstone and Rocky Mountain National Parks and Wikimedia Commons, public domain or CC0 only. The calls were cut from the recordings by `tools/audio/cut.py`, which finds what stands over the quiet of a recording; `tools/audio/cut.sh` holds every cut so it can be made again. OpenGameArt refuses automated fetching and was left alone.
  - *Not done, and not known.* Nobody has listened. The night's bed is still one eleven-second recording (no other free of conditions was found), under made crickets (`make.sh`: tone pulses), which may sound made. The cuts were chosen by level: one may hold two birds, or half a phrase. The birds are North American and European kinds together. Sets still of one or two files that are heard often: `swim` (1), `splash` (2), `swing`, `hit`, `hurt` (2 each), and most animals' voices (a horse, a deer and a boar have one recording each).

- **Every kind has a sound of its own** (2026-11-11). Eight had another's (fox and lynx the wolf's, moose the deer's, ogre the troll's, badger the boar's, squirrel and hedgehog the rabbit's, the giant toad the frog's). Moose, hedgehog, toad and ogre now have recordings from Commons (CC0 and public domain); for fox, lynx, badger and squirrel nothing free of conditions was found there, and they have voiced imitations from a CC0 pack, chosen by measured length and pitch. Where a recording is still of an animal of another size (the giant toad's is a hand-long toad's) it is pitched to the animal: (recorded height / its own)^0.35, between 0.7 and 1.5. Each is played at (recorded animal's height / its own)^0.35, held between 0.7 and 1.5, so a fox is a fifth higher than a wolf and a moose a tenth lower than a deer. Beaver, crab, lizard and tortoise, which had none, have sounds made here (`tools/audio/make.sh`: a tail on the water, claws, a hiss, a grunt), as has the dragonfly's wing; the hawks overhead have a hawk's call (CC0, Commons) where they had a crow's; swallows call as the small birds do, and a leaping fish is heard to splash. A butterfly is silent, as it is. Not done: recordings of their own for those that share, and nobody has listened to the made ones.
- **Mixed by ear, in the admin panel** (`audio::Mix`, F1: air, creatures' voices, footfalls). Nothing here can listen; the developer did, and found the birds in his face, the ambience and footfalls bad and the river like a drain. What was done without ears: all three kinds turned down by default; a bird's call is never played from nearer than twelve metres and comes less often; running water is heard within nine metres of it, not twenty-two. The water loop and the footfalls were then replaced by recordings of the thing itself (a shallow stony river, public domain; steps on soft ground and on stone, CC0): the first water loop came from a pack of splash effects and the first steps from a pack of made sounds, which is what the ear had said. `tools/audio/steady.py` measures how even a loop is and how much of it is low rumble; the wind was all under 300 Hz, a rumble, and now has a band of made noise over it (300 to 1700 Hz, swelling in gusts that fit the loop), so 15% of it is low. None of this has been listened to since.

Built 2026-11-11 (`cube_game::audio`); the files are CC0, in `assets/audio`, whose README gives each one's source.

- **A sound is a set of files found by name**: `wolf` is `wolf_1.ogg` and `wolf_2.ogg`, one is picked and played a little off its pitch. A creature's voice is the name of a set, in `[life] voice` of its `creature.toml`.
- **The air of a place is the ecology's** (`ecology::ambience`, tested): wind by how open and high it is, birdsong by day where there are trees and little in winter, insects on warm nights, water by nearness to it. (At first four loops played always, their loudness eased to it; see the first entry for what replaced them.)
- **A creature is heard when the simulation says it does something**: its voice with a blow, a hit when wounded, a death; and its call now and then at the hours its niche says it is about. Heard from where it is, to 45 m. The player's feet sound for each stride by what is underfoot (grass, stone, shallows), with a splash going in and a stroke for each while swimming.
- **The small birds and the ones overhead are heard from where they are** (`air::Sings`, `audio::singers`): each now and then, only while it is out.
- **Checked without listening**: every sound played is a line of the trace (`sound step_grass_2 vol=0.58 at 9,12,6`), so a scripted `--play --scene` run can be read for what was heard. `--quiet` for none.
- **Levels are measured, not heard** (`tools/audio/level.py`): every one-shot is brought to a mean of -20 dB and every loop to -27 dB, no peak over -1 dB; 37 of the 52 files were out, by up to 7 dB. Run it after adding a file.
- **Loops join their own start, by measurement** (`tools/audio/loops.py`): the jump from a loop's last sample to its first must be no more than is usual between neighbouring samples inside it; one of the six failed and had its last second faded into its first.
- **Not known:** how any of it sounds. The files were chosen by title and length by an agent that cannot hear, levels are not matched, and several loops' seams are unheard. A person must listen and prune. No fox bark or true wolf howl was to be had under CC0.

## Ecology

Built 2026-11-11. One system decides what life a place has (`cube_sim::ecology`); `cargo world life` judges it.

- **A place is described once, for everything that might live there** (`Place`): its biome, metres to the nearest water, how deep the water is, tree cover from open sky to deep wood (the trees within eight metres), height over the sea. `Ecology::new` measures the island for it every two metres.
- **A kind of life has a niche** (`Niche`): the biomes it lives in and how well each suits it, how near water it keeps (`edge`, `near`, `away`, `in`), how much cover it wants (`open`, `edge`, `deep`), and when it is about (`day`, `night`, `dusk`). `Niche::fit(place, hour)` is 0 to 1.
- **Everything that puts life in the world asks it.** Animals: `[life]` in `creature.toml` carries `habitat`, `water`, `cover` and `hours`, and `Sim::populate` settles a group where its niche fits, the rare kinds first. Small life the game draws: butterflies, songbirds, swallows, dragonflies, fireflies, fish, falling leaves each have a niche in `ecology.rs`, and `air.rs` places every one through `settles`. A firefly is in damp woodland within reach of water from dusk; near the camera only if the camera is somewhere like that. Before, each kind had its own rule of the form "near the camera, on any dry ground".
- **Why in the simulation:** where things live is a rule of the world, and the same answer must hold for a deer that can be hunted and a butterfly that is only seen.
- **Winter thins what is about** (`Niche::cold`, an animal's `winter` in `[life]`): in the depth of it there are no butterflies, dragonflies, fireflies or swallows, fewer songbirds, and the bear, hedgehog, frog, toad, tortoise and lizard mostly lie up. By early spring all are back.
- **There is weather** (`sky::cloud`, `sky::rain`, `sky::snows`, 2026-11-14). How much of the sky is cloud goes with the same slow weather that drives the wind (a blowing day is a clouded one) on a slower swell of its own; it rains only under heavy cloud, in showers within a wet spell. Over a year: clear three fifths of the time, overcast a fifth, raining an eighth, on two days in five, and neither ever jumps (a test holds those). In the rain what lives on dry ground is about a quarter as much as its hours would have it (`Sim::step`), so it lies up, goes to its den or up to roost; what lives in the water or at its edge takes no notice.
- **Not done:** (Creatures eat, drink, breed and die, and the seasons turn: [What lives in it](#what-lives-in-it). The ogre has a home again, on the snow: `cargo world life`.)

**What grazes follows the grass** (`Sim::day`, 2026-11-13): a plant-eater whose piece of grazing is nearly bare (under 0.4 of 1) moves its home to the best of the eight pieces beside it that is its kind of ground and has 0.2 more; all that share a home find the same piece, so a herd stays one. Before, it stayed and starved: a moose eats three deer's worth, bared its own piece, and on five seeds as many starved as were born (28 fell to 0 to 10 in 24 years; now 40 to 60). `cargo world years` prints births and each cause of death for any kind that has halved, and how many followed the grass. A few solitary hunters are the fragile kinds: hawk and lynx keep rock and found too little to hunt within a walker's range of it (`range = 300` and room for more), and spider lairs reared 0.4 a year (`litter = 1.0`); with those, seeds 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 all pass 24 years (2026-11-13). Change a `[life]` against all five.

## A big world

Built 2026-11-11: the island is a kilometre across (`terrain::RADIUS`), and only the part round the player is held in cells.

- **The lie of the land is known for all of it, always** (`Land::new`, a second or two on every core): each column's height and biome, and, decided from the land alone, where every tree stands, of what kind and how tall, and how big each rock is. So the ecology, the far land and the tools can speak of the whole island though almost none of it is made.
- **Cells are made a region at a time** (`terrain::fill`; a region is 32 m square): the ground, what stands on it, the ground cover. The same call makes the same cells whenever and in whatever order it is asked, which is what lets a region be dropped and made again. A tree whose crown reaches in from outside is grown with the region but writes only that region's columns (`plants::only`): written whole, every tree near an edge was written by each region near it, and four fifths of a region's 260 ms was other regions' trees (now 70 ms).
- **The game makes regions within 176 m of the camera and drops them beyond 240 m** (`world::regions`), nearest first; a region someone has dug or built in is kept, since nothing is saved yet. About 1.3 GB held, as the whole of the old 384 m island was.
- **Nothing long is done on the frame's thread** (2026-11-11). A region's cells, a chunk's mesh and a region's trees are each worked out on the engine's task threads (most of the machine's: `TaskPoolPlugin` in main.rs) and the frame only takes in what is finished: a region as a world of its own joined to the rest (`Voxels::join`), a mesh from a copy of the chunks round it (`Voxels::around`; chunks are shared and copied on write). A chunk beside ground not yet made is not meshed until it is, or its mesh is thrown away when the ground arrives. Looking for what to mesh is itself every chunk there is, so it is a pass spread over sixteen frames. Before this the 208 m view took 55 s to be ready and a frame in which a region arrived took up to 490 ms; after, 5 s and 54 ms at worst. `make pace` holds it to bars; `cargo world cost` says what a region and a mesh cost by part; `SLOW_TRACE=1` with a scripted strip lists every slow frame and what was made in it.
- **A frame is as short in every kind of place** (2026-11-13, `tools/pace.sh --at X,Z` where `cargo world who` has a forest, rock, a shore and snow, on a Radeon 8060S): the middle frame 9.0, 4.7, 8.4 and 4.0 ms, one in a hundred over 18.2, 12.1, 16.5 and 12.2, the worst 30, 31, 41 and 25. `make pace` runs one meadow, and that had been the only place measured. (In the forest and on the shore the trial says FAIL for how far he ran: he met a bank and the water. Its frames passed.)
- **Beyond the cells, the far land** (`cube_game::far`): the whole island as a light mesh from the height map, a region to a piece and four metres between points, with a plain shape of the right kind and height for each tree and a sheet of water over what is wet. A piece is hidden when its region's real ground is there and near. A mountain two valleys off is there to be walked to.
- **Creatures are lived only near the watcher** (`Sim::focus`, `life::AWAKE` = 110 m): the island has some two thousand, a couple of hundred awake at a time; the rest are as they were left, and are stood clear of whatever is there when they wake. The game gives a model only to the ones awake (`motion::mirror`).
- **A bigger island has more mountains**: two near the start as before, and two more for every 384 m square of island, placed and sized by the seed.
- **The tools judge what the game has**: `cargo world` makes the whole island's land and the cells within 160 m of the start, and lives the creatures near a person there.
- **Not done**: saving (so dropped regions forget nothing only because edited ones are kept); the far land has no rocks or undergrowth and its trees are plain shapes; the land itself is still one island held as one array (64 MB), not made as it is come to: ten kilometres wants `Land` in pieces too.

**The slowest frame of a timed run was the picture's, not the game's** (2026-11-13). Every `make pace` run had one frame of 50 to 70 ms at the same moment. It was not the day's turn, a region taken in, a first jump, or any system of ours (`SLOW_TRACE`, `crate::Slow`): the engine's own trace (`--features bevy/trace_chrome`, `TRACE_CHROME=file`, 2 GB for ten seconds) had 41 ms of it in `PipelineCache::process_pipeline_queue_system`, making ready what a new kind of thing is drawn with, on the frame it first came into view. A scene does that on the frame so no picture lacks anything; the game does it off the frame. `--as-played` has a scene do as the game does, and `make pace` uses it: worst frame 27 to 42 ms in three runs. Profile before a fourth guess.

## Seeing far

Started 2026-11-10, when the island was drawn whole at full detail and hazed out from 80 m.

- **Three levels of detail a chunk, chosen by distance** (`world::LEVELS`): every cell to 40 m; cells taken two a side to 104 m (a quarter of the faces); four a side beyond (a sixteenth). `mesh_chunk_far` makes the coarse ones: a group of cells is solid if any is, and is the block highest in it, so distant ground has no holes and trees keep their bulk; ground cover and the bits of broken cells are left out.
- **Bevy's `VisibilityRange` does the switching**, with a fade of 8 m between levels so nothing pops. Every level of a chunk is drawn from an entity at the chunk's middle, with its vertices given from there: the fade is computed from the entity's origin, and with all chunks at the world's origin every one faded as if it stood there (the first try: half the island ghosted, the rest missing). `use_aabb` is not for crossfades.
- **Haze from 180 m to 700 m**, so the island is clear from end to end and the sea has no edge.
- **A chunk's level is meshed when the camera first comes near enough to need it** (`world::stream`), nearest first, in a slice of each frame (4 ms; 50 ms for the first three seconds and for scenes), and dropped when far behind. The island doubled in width on that. Still to do, in order:
  1. Meshing off the main thread.
  2. Merge flat faces of one colour (greedy meshing). The per-cell tint has to move out of the vertices first (a small noise texture or a shader), or nothing merges.
  3. Make and keep chunks only near the player, the rest as the seed and what has been changed: then the world is as big as wanted.
  4. Beyond the last chunks, one coarse mesh drawn from `Land`'s height map and biome colours, to the horizon: mountains seen from kilometres off cost almost nothing.
- **Judged by** `cargo world survey`: the faces drawn standing at the start, each chunk at the level its distance gives it (bar: 2 million).

## Creatures

Full detail in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md). The design choices:

- **Creatures are stylised low-poly skinned meshes, not voxel models.** Eco does the same. The world is blocks; what lives in it is not. Tried the other way on 2026-11-10 and decided against: creatures rebuilt from the world's 5 cm cubes (`cargo forge cubes`) can look right standing still when the model is drawn for it, but took a dozen rounds to get one figure there, come apart at the joints in motion, lose small animals altogether, and would mean buying every creature again. A creature should belong to the world by its style (chunky shapes, flat colour, a shared palette), not by being made of the same cubes. The trial, its checks and what it showed are kept in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#cubes). Three other looks were then drawn and two built beside the house style (carved from blocks, flat-shaded, hand-painted; [pipeline.md](pipeline.md#trying-a-look)), and the house style was kept: faceted, bulky, flat colour.
- **Tripo makes mesh, texture, skeleton and skin weights. We make most of the motion.** Tripo has a dozen preset clips for bipeds and exactly one (a walk) for anything else, so animals' other clips are made in code against bone *roles* read from the skeleton's shape. One recipe serves every species; Spore did the same.
- **Clips are baked into the model file by forge, not computed in the game.** A baked clip can be checked by numbers and seen on a sheet before it ships, and the game just plays glTF. The same Rust recipes can move into the game later for what must react at runtime (feet on slopes, looking at things).
- **The player is moved by the two-legged walker as the game runs, not by clips** (`cube_anim::biped`, `cube_game::stride`, 2026-11-12). The order that got there, which is the order for anything new: the arithmetic and its tests, then a figure of boxes (`make person`), then the model. Each frame the skeleton is stood as forge says it rests (`[stride]` in `meta.toml`) and each bone turned to where the walker has it, so a foot that is down stays on the ground that is there at any speed, and starting, stopping, turning, a slope and a leap are the walker's own with nothing mixed between clips. What the walker does not do stays a clip's: a blow or a flinch from the chest up over the walker's legs (so a blow can be struck at a run), dying and swimming the whole body. Clips carried from the library reached the model pose for pose (set side by side), so the carrying was never the fault; what was unnatural was playing recorded strides at speeds and on ground they were not made for. `--clips-only` turns it off, to compare.
- **One skeleton for every biped** (Tripo's Mixamo-named rig, 65 joints). The player and humanoid monsters share bone names, so clips and gear are shared.
- **Player customisation, in order of cost** (none built yet): tint and texture swaps on the one base body; rigid gear (helmet, pauldrons, weapons) parented to a single bone, which needs no skin weights and so suits generated meshes; body-part swaps on the shared skeleton only if those are not enough. Generated *skinned* clothing is avoided: fitting it to a body is the hard problem terra spent weeks on.

## Moving through the world

- **What stops a body at a tree is the wood that is drawn** (2026-11-11; the developer: "trees should have more accurate colliders, ideally literally the voxels you can see"). Within 2.5 m of a tree's foot every limb is written into the world in the very cubes it is drawn in near the eye (`Limb::near`: 10 cm, 15 for a great trunk), each cube as the 5 cm bits it covers; above that, in cells as before. By cells a body went up to 20 cm into wood it could see and was stopped 16 cm short of it; `cargo world trunks` now finds 0 cm at the middle and 7 cm at the edges of a hundred. A broken cell's bits are held by its chunk (they were one map for the world, and every mesh copied the whole of it). It costs a region 100 ms more to make (170, on a task thread) and some 0.4 MB. Found on the way: a leaning trunk's slices lean with it, and in fine cubes a great one was hollow past a third of its radius.
- **Feet are put on the ground that is there** (`cube_game::feet`, 2026-11-11). A clip is made for flat ground; on a hillside the standing foot was up to 28 cm off the ground drawn under it. After the clip has posed the body and before the pose is used, each leg is bent, two bones and the knee the way it already bends, so its foot is as far above the ground under it as the clip had it above the level; the body comes down as far as its lowest foot must reach; a foot is moved at most 17% of the creature's height. Which bones are a leg forge says for every creature alike (`leg0`… in `meta.toml`, from the roles it reads: nothing in the game knows a biped from a deer; a leg whose bones share a name with another bone is left out). Only for what stands on the ground within 30 m of the eye. `make feet` measures it: player 27.6 cm to 3.8, bear 27.9 to 1.6, fox 24.9 to 3.3, wolf 6.4 to 0.6. What goes on four legs is first pitched to the slope under it (from its hind feet to its fore, up to 24°, about the bone it hangs from; which way it is long is read off the feet), so its legs have only the unevenness left: on a steep bank the deer went from 49 cm to 0.2, the pony from 42 to 0.2. **A bone moved after the clip is put back before it is moved again** (`Legs::moved`): a clip does not pose every bone every frame (a standing animal's root has no track), and moved again from where it was left, a deer standing on a slope sank further each frame and the pitch ran away. Not done: a foot does not turn to lie along the slope; the ground's height under a foot is not eased, so a leg can snap as its foot crosses from one block to the next (eased as a height from the body it lagged a climb by 20 cm).
- **The camera rides a height of its own** (`player::follow`, 2026-11-11): it comes after the body's, never more than half a metre behind, and ground in the way pulls it in quickly and lets it out slowly. Fixed to the body it took each block of the ground as a jolt: a scripted run now says how it rode (`camera worst-step, p99`), and the one-in-a-hundred jolt went from 24 cm to 2.5. `CAMERA_RAW=1` is the old way, to set beside it.
- **The model comes up a block's step, it is not put on it** (`motion::mirror`): where the ground is drawn in blocks, feet (and the eyes that ride on them) ease to each new level over a tenth of a second, never more than 35 cm behind. Put straight on each block as it was reached, walking was a string of jolts.

Built 2026-11-10. The rules are `cube_sim::body`; the game only feeds it keys and draws the result.

- **Our own box-against-voxels movement, not a physics engine.** A body is an upright box moved one axis at a time and put back against the cell face it crossed. A block world needs nothing more, it is about 150 lines, it runs headless, and it will behave the same on a server. A physics engine (Avian, Rapier) would bring its own world to keep in step with ours and is not deterministic across machines by default.
- **A ledge of half a metre is walked up, and down, without leaving the ground**: two cells. Every hill is a staircase of cells; needing a jump for each would be unplayable. The model eases after the body over a step so it does not pop.
- **Fixed tick, drawn in between.** Bodies step 60 times a second whatever the frame rate; the game draws them between the last two ticks.
- **Third person, camera-relative.** The models are the point of the game, so the player sees theirs. WASD is relative to the camera, the character turns to face where it goes, and ground between camera and character pulls the camera in (a ray through the voxels).
- **Every moving creature has the same body.** The keys drive one; a wander drives the rest; the player is not special. So an animal steps, falls and swims by the same rules, and any creature can be controlled (`--as wolf`).
- **Creatures do not pass through each other.** Each tick (`Sim::step`), before bodies step, overlapping ones are pushed apart over the ground (`body::separate`): two movers share the push, one standing still gives none. A creature is met as a capsule sized from its measured standing width and length (`meta.toml`): round for an upright figure, a fattened line along the body for an animal, so a wolf is bumped at its nose and not only its middle. Rounded, so they slide past instead of locking corners. `--bodies`, or the panel's tick box, draws the shapes. Against the voxel world a body is still a square box of the capsule's radius, so a long animal's head can poke into a wall.
- **The gait is asked for, and moves at its clip's speed.** Walk, run or sprint is the driver's choice (Ctrl, nothing, Shift) and the body moves at the speed forge built or measured that clip for, so feet hold: 1.2, 4.0 and 5.6 m/s for the player. While speeding up or slowing the clip's rate follows the body's speed.
- **In the air and in water the body picks the clip:** `jump` on the way up from a jump, `fall` otherwise, `land` after a drop of more than about half a metre if it is not running on, `swim` when the water is over its middle. All are made by forge ([pipeline.md](pipeline.md)).
- **Feel, in numbers** (`Tuning`): a run is reached in under a fifth of a second (18 m/s²) and stopped in an eighth (26); gravity 22 m/s²; a held jump clears a metre and a half and a tapped one is a hop of a third of that; a body turns to face the way it is going at ten radians a second, not in one tick; a jump pressed up to 0.12 s before landing still happens, and up to 0.1 s after walking off an edge; falls are capped at 25 m/s, and a body that ends a tick inside the floor is put back on top of it. The model leans a few degrees into turns and into speeding up.
- **Water:** a body floats with the surface at its middle, swims up with the jump key and down with Ctrl. Water is drawn as its own see-through mesh.
- **First person is the same camera at the eyes** with the model hidden and turned to face where the camera looks (V). Third person stays the default.
- **Feet are not placed on steps.** A body eases over a step in about a fifth of a second and its feet do not seek the riser. Other block games do the same; leg IK against voxels in the game is real work for little gain while every hill is a staircase. Shaped blocks (slopes) change that sum, so it is decided again then.
- **Not yet:** arms or body visible in first person, a camera and tint for under water, turning on the spot (the model only turns as it moves, or with the camera in first person).

## The sandbox and agent tooling

- **A script's times are on the picture's clock** (`shot::Stage::ready_for`, 2026-11-11): seconds since the scene was ready to be pictured, not since the program started. As the world grew (more ground, trees as cubes, a hundred models in the air) it took longer to be ready than a script took to run, so every `--strip` and `--gif` was of someone standing still after the script was over, and an hour went on a walk animation that was not broken. `ANIM_TRACE=player` (with any `--scene --play`) prints what a creature's clip is doing.

- `cargo world` judges the world and its creatures with no window, in under two seconds: `survey` (the land), `run` (five minutes of life, a line a kind, then `ok` or `FAIL` lines), `map` (one picture from above with where everything went), `who` (where each group lives). `WORLD_TRACE=deer cargo world run` follows the deer that ends up most stuck, a line a second, with the ground round it when it is caught. This is where behaviour and terrain are changed and checked; the window is for how it looks.
- `cargo sandbox` is the debug level: spawn any creature, play any clip, scrub, wander, give it its own mind, show skeletons. `--wild` starts the living world instead.
- `--scene FILE --wild --at X,Z` pictures any place on the island (`--view high` from the air), so a herd or a hillside can be looked at without walking there.
- `cargo sandbox --shot ID` draws offscreen to a PNG with no window, so an agent on a server can look. A sheet is one image: rows are clips, columns are moments.
- `--scene FILE --play --script '…' --strip 10` runs a scripted route with no window and saves it as a strip of pictures: how movement is judged without a screen. `--trace` prints the body.
- `--gif 12` saves the same run as a moving picture. A strip is for the agent, which reads numbers and one still; a GIF is for the person, who can see in a second whether a change of clip pops or a gait feels wrong, and the agent does not have to look at it.
- The real window runs on a machine with no GPU screen through the software Vulkan driver, and `tools/drive/drive.py` presses keys and moves the mouse in it. That is how the keyboard, mouse look and cursor lock were tested ([development.md](development.md)).
- The window opens on a loading screen when someone is to play in it (`cube_game::loading`), because the world is made a region at a time and the first seconds were spent standing in half of one. It lifts when everything within 60 m of the camera is made (the regions' cells, the chunks' meshes, the regions' trees, the models of the player and of what is awake), and its bar is those counts. Behind it the world is held still rather than hidden: the keys do nothing, the simulation only wakes what is near (`Sim::wake`: no time passes, nothing moves or is hurt) and the sound is turned down to nothing, so nobody is bitten before they can see. While it is up nearly the whole frame goes to making the world (0.12 s each to regions and to meshes; a bar needs only a few frames a second). Twenty seconds with nothing new made and it lifts anyway and says why: a model that fails to load must not be a game that never starts. It is not in a `--scene`, which does its own waiting. It prints `loading done t= regions= meshes= barred=` once, and `make window` judges it: a W pressed while it is up is counted as turned away and moves nobody.
- Wanted next: a remote port (Bevy Remote Protocol) so an agent can drive a running sandbox.
